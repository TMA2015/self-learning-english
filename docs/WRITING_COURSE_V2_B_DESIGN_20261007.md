## 1. Scope

WCV2-B authors the academic content and interaction model for Weeks 2–6 only.

Weeks:
- Week 2 — Brainstorming & Relevance
- Week 3 — Idea Development & Reasons
- Week 4 — Examples & Details
- Week 5 — Sentence Clarity & Linkers
- Week 6 — Outlining & Response Structure

Weeks 7–12 remain preview-only.

This phase must continue the WCV2-A principles:
- self-learning first;
- AI optional;
- deterministic feedback limited to what can be checked safely;
- progressive help;
- learner writes before seeing examples;
- scaffolding begins to fade.

## 2. Shared lesson rhythm

Every week follows:

1. Learn
2. Guided Practice
3. Main Practice
4. Feedback & Revision
5. Review

Each week should take about 25–35 minutes total and may be split into two sessions.

## 3. Shared feedback principles

### During active work
Allowed:
- explanation;
- guiding question;
- short hint;
- sentence starter / example piece;
- deterministic checks for objective items.

Not allowed:
- full model answer;
- complete rewritten learner response;
- CEFR claim from rule-based checks;
- semantic-quality claim from keyword matching.

### Final Review
After learner submits their own attempt:
- model comparison may be revealed explicitly;
- learner compares structure, not copies wording.

## 4. Evidence policy

### Guided Practice
Objective items:
- evidence weight 1;
- proficiency weight 0;
- skill-map practice evidence only.

### Main Practice free-form
- deterministic proficiency weight 0;
- progress/completion evidence only;
- language quality remains provisional unless evaluated by valid AI/adult/rubric process.

## 5. Week 2 — Brainstorming & Relevance

### Goal
Learner can generate several ideas, reject off-topic ideas, and select one or two relevant ideas that directly answer the prompt.

### Foundation link
Build Ideas

### Learn
Teach three ideas:
1. **Generate first** — write several possible ideas before choosing.
2. **Check relevance** — ask: “Does this idea answer the exact task?”
3. **Select stronger ideas** — prefer ideas that can be explained with a reason or example.

Annotated example prompt:

> Your school wants one new lunchtime club. Suggest a club and explain why students would enjoy it.

Possible ideas:
- drawing club — relevant;
- chess club — relevant;
- changing the school uniform — off-topic;
- music club — relevant.

Explain why “changing the school uniform” is off-topic.

Common mistake:
Choosing the first idea without checking whether it can be explained.

### Guided Practice
Use a different prompt:

> Your class wants one activity for a rainy afternoon. Choose an activity and give one reason.

Objective items:
1. identify a relevant idea;
2. identify an off-topic idea;
3. choose which of two ideas is easier to explain;
4. match an idea with a suitable reason.

Suggested mini-game:
**Off-Topic Spotter**

### Main Practice
Prompt:

> Your school can add one new after-school activity. What activity should it add? Give reasons.

Learner fields:
- My possible ideas
- Off-topic idea to reject
- Best main idea
- Why this idea fits the task

### Deterministic helper boundaries
Allowed:
- field completion;
- whether learner selected one of their listed ideas;
- explicit presence of a reason connector if required.

Not allowed:
- deciding whether a free-form idea is truly relevant unless it matches a predetermined objective option.

### Review
Learner finishes:
- “A relevant idea answers …”
- “I rejected … because …”
- “My strongest idea is …”

## 6. Week 3 — Idea Development & Reasons

### Goal
Learner can turn an opinion or selected idea into a clear reason and supporting explanation.

### Foundation link
Develop Ideas

### Learn
Teach:
**Point → Why → Explanation**

Example:
- Point: Schools should have a reading club.
- Why: It helps students discover new books.
- Explanation: Students can share recommendations and talk about stories together.

Common mistake:
Repeating the point instead of explaining why.

Bad:
“A reading club is good because it is a good club.”

Better:
“A reading club is useful because students can discover books they might not choose alone.”

### Guided Practice
Prompt:

> Students should have ten minutes of quiet reading at school.

Objective items:
1. choose a real reason rather than a repeated opinion;
2. identify which sentence explains “why”;
3. match a point to a logical reason;
4. choose which explanation develops the reason.

### Main Practice
Prompt:

> Your class should have one short outdoor learning activity each week. Do you agree? Give reasons.

Learner fields:
- My opinion / point
- Reason 1
- Why this reason matters
- Another supporting detail

### Deterministic helper boundaries
Allowed:
- all fields completed;
- obvious exact repetition detection;
- explicit reason connector presence.

Not allowed:
- judging whether the reason is logically strong from free text.

### Review
- “My point is …”
- “My reason explains why because …”
- “I added this detail …”

## 7. Week 4 — Examples & Details

### Goal
Learner can support a reason with a specific example or useful detail.

### Foundation link
Develop Ideas

### Learn
Teach:
**Reason → Detail → Example**

Example:
- Reason: A school garden teaches responsibility.
- Detail: Students must remember to care for plants regularly.
- Example: One group could water tomato plants every Friday and record their growth.

Common mistake:
Giving an example that does not support the reason.

### Guided Practice
Prompt:

> Students should help keep their classroom tidy.

Objective items:
1. choose a detail that supports a reason;
2. choose the best example;
3. identify an unrelated example;
4. match reason → detail → example.

### Main Practice
Prompt:

> Students should spend some class time learning practical life skills. Do you agree? Give a reason and an example.

Learner fields:
- My reason
- Supporting detail
- Specific example
- How the example supports the reason

### Deterministic helper boundaries
Allowed:
- completion;
- explicit example marker presence;
- objective matching items.

Not allowed:
- claiming an example is semantically strong from keyword overlap alone.

### Review
- “My reason is …”
- “My example shows …”
- “The example connects to my reason because …”

## 8. Week 5 — Sentence Clarity & Linkers

### Goal
Learner can write one clear idea per sentence and connect closely related ideas using simple linkers.

### Foundation link
Write Clear Sentences

### Learn
Teach:
- one main thought per sentence;
- use **because** for reason;
- use **so** for result;
- use **but** for contrast;
- use **although** for simple contrast when ready.

Examples:
- We stayed inside because it was raining.
- It was raining, so we read in the library.
- The task was difficult, but we finished it together.

Common mistakes:
- joining too many ideas in one sentence;
- using a linker without a complete idea on both sides where required;
- choosing a linker whose relationship does not match the meaning.

### Guided Practice
Objective items:
1. choose because vs so;
2. choose but for contrast;
3. identify a run-on sentence;
4. combine two short sentences with an appropriate linker.

### Main Practice
Prompt:

> Describe a school activity you enjoy and explain why you enjoy it.

Learner writes:
- 3–5 connected sentences;
- at least one reason;
- at least one useful linker.

### Deterministic helper boundaries
Allowed:
- sentence count heuristic;
- explicit linker presence;
- punctuation heuristic;
- objective linker-choice items.

Not allowed:
- certifying grammar correctness;
- certifying sentence meaning.

### Review
- “I used ___ to show a reason/result/contrast.”
- “One sentence I improved was …”

## 9. Week 6 — Outlining & Response Structure

### Goal
Learner can organize a short response before drafting.

### Foundation link
Organize Writing

### Learn
Teach a four-part short-response plan:

1. Opening / opinion
2. Reason
3. Detail or example
4. Ending

For longer responses, learners may repeat Reason → Detail/Example.

Example outline:
- Opening: I think schools should offer a reading club.
- Reason: It helps students discover new books.
- Example: Members can recommend one book each month.
- Ending: A reading club would make lunchtime more interesting.

Common mistake:
Writing full polished sentences too early and losing the plan.

### Guided Practice
Objective items:
1. place outline parts in order;
2. identify which line belongs in the opening;
3. identify which line is an example;
4. remove one unrelated outline point.

### Main Practice
Prompt:

> Your school has money for one improvement. What should it choose? Explain your answer.

Learner fields:
- Opening / opinion
- Reason
- Supporting detail
- Example
- Ending

### Deterministic helper boundaries
Allowed:
- field completion;
- ordering;
- whether required structural components exist.

Not allowed:
- judging the quality of the argument;
- grading language proficiency.

### Review
- “My response begins with …”
- “My main support is …”
- “My ending reminds the reader …”

## 10. Scaffolding fade across Weeks 2–6

Week 2:
high support — explicit brainstorming fields and relevance prompts.

Week 3:
high-medium support — Point / Why / Explanation.

Week 4:
medium support — Reason / Detail / Example connection.

Week 5:
medium-low support — learner writes connected sentences rather than filling all planning fields.

Week 6:
planning support returns in compact outline form, but content choices belong to learner.

The learner should gradually move from selecting/checking ideas to producing connected original text.

## 14. Acceptance criteria

Academic:
- progression from ideas → reasons → examples → sentences → outline is coherent;
- tasks are age-appropriate for 10–14;
- language is suitable for A2 to early B1;
- examples are original and simple;
- no IELTS band claims.

Self-learning:
- every week teaches before asking for independent work;
- objective Guided Practice explains answers;
- Main Practice remains learner-authored;
- feedback says what to improve without completing the task;
- full models remain locked until Review.

Technical/pedagogical safety:
- deterministic checks remain Basic Structure only;
- free-form proficiency weight remains 0 without valid review;
- no AI dependency;
- WCV2-A local-save and model-lock rules remain intact.
