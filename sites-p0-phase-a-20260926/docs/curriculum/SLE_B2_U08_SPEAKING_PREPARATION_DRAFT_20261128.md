## CORE-1 — Situation and goal
You are the student representative at a school activities committee. Another student has missed a discussion and asks what the group actually agreed. Prepare a brief, balanced oral explanation for that student and adjust your language for a different audience. By the end you can:
- organise a spoken briefing around the issue, two differing positions, a qualified decision and unresolved action;
- choose neutral reporting language that preserves uncertainty;
- use a keyword outline to rehearse without reading a memorised script; adapt phrasing to the listener and self-check for fidelity.

## CORE-2 — Toolkit and independent model
### Idea bank
What needs deciding? Who favours which option and why? What limits the evidence? Where did the group find common ground? What was **not** agreed? What should the absent listener know or do next?

### Functional language toolkit
| Purpose | Useful language | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Open the briefing | “The main question was whether…” | A question is not a decision. |
| Report a view | “One member favoured…, mainly because…” | Don't strengthen a tentative view into certainty. |
| Preserve a concern | “Another member was open to the idea, provided that…” | Conditional support is not full agreement. |
| Compare perspectives | “Both were concerned about…, but they differed on…” | Mention shared ground only when supported. |
| Clarify a decision | “For now, the group agreed only to…” | Use “approved” only for a genuine approval. |
| State remaining action | “Before anything is confirmed, … needs to be checked.” | Avoid inventing deadlines or responsibilities. |
| Adapt to audience | “What this means for us is…” / “The outstanding issue is…” | Change register and detail, not facts. |

### Worked model (distinct scenario — not the active task)
**Model source:** A garden committee discusses a weekend planting session. Eva favours Saturday because more volunteers might be available; Rui says the tools cannot be collected until Monday. Both agree to ask the site manager whether a smaller weekday session can be arranged. No session is booked.

**Keyword plan:** planting session → Eva: possible Saturday availability → Rui: tools Monday → ask manager about smaller weekday session → nothing booked.

**Annotated model for a fellow volunteer:**
“Just to update you, the group was discussing when to organise the planting session. Eva thought Saturday might suit more volunteers, while Rui pointed out that the tools couldn't be collected until Monday. They agreed to ask the site manager about a smaller weekday session. That's all we've decided so far — there isn't a booking yet.”

Why it works: the opening identifies the issue, the middle attributes distinct views, “might” preserves Eva's uncertainty, and the ending separates an enquiry from approval. A misleading version would be “Everyone voted for Monday”: the source does not support a vote or a definite Monday session. For a staff member, the opening could be “The committee discussed possible scheduling options.” This changes register without changing the decision. The model uses a garden scenario, not the assessed practice scenario.

## CORE-3 — Independent practice (all unscored)
### New written meeting record: a student exhibition guide
The school exhibition is planning how to help visitors find student projects. Imani favours a printed map, explaining that some families may prefer to have something they can carry. Felix supports testing a digital guide but worries that a guide requiring a phone may not be convenient for every visitor. A volunteer says that a brief trial of the digital guide attracted twelve comments; nine commenters liked its layout. No visitor survey was conducted, and no one recorded how many visitors used the guide. Imani says she could support testing both formats if the printing cost stays within the approved budget. Felix agrees that it would be useful to compare the formats, but says the school first needs to check accessibility and cost. The group agrees to ask the exhibition coordinator for a cost estimate and accessibility advice. It does not approve either format or announce a launch date.

**Your task — Audience A:** a student volunteer who missed the meeting. Prepare a 60–90-second spoken briefing. Make keyword notes (not a full script): issue; Imani's view and condition; Felix's view and concern; the scope of the feedback; agreed next step and what remains undecided. Then speak aloud using your notes; do not copy the model. No recording or upload is required.

**Your task — Audience B:** an exhibition coordinator who needs to know what action to consider. Rehearse a shorter, more formal update (about 30–45 seconds). Keep the same material facts but foreground the requested next step and unresolved constraints. A reasonable variation in length is fine; timing is a planning aid, not a test of fluency.

**Optional practice:** Ask a neutral clarifying question you could use if a listener said, “So the digital guide has been approved?” Rehearse an accurate reply. Do not assert a decision not in the record.

### Learner action (paper or editable notes; no implementation implied)
1. Fill a five-line keyword plan from the new record.
2. Speak aloud once without reading the worked model.
3. Adapt the plan for Audience B; rehearse again.
4. Record only a self-report of what you tried, which point was difficult and one improvement. Don't record private voices or invent a score. 

## CORE-4 — Self-check and repair (after rehearsal)
| Check | If not yet satisfied |
|---|---|
| I identified the real question, not an invented approved plan. | Return to the first and last sentences of the record. |
| I represented Imani and Felix distinctly and preserved their conditions. | Add speaker labels and a short “provided that” / “but” note. |
| I reported nine of twelve voluntary commenters, not nine of twelve visitors or universal support. | Recheck what the numbers measure; avoid claiming participation was measured. |
| I reported an enquiry for cost and accessibility advice, not a launch decision. | Mark “agreed to ask” separately from “not approved”. |
| I changed emphasis/register for the coordinator without changing facts. | Remove chatty opening or foreground the requested action, then rehearse. |
| I spoke from keywords rather than copying a complete model script. | Shorten notes and try again; no penalty or claimed fluency measurement. |

**Feedback boundaries:** this checklist supports self-monitoring. It cannot measure fluency, pronunciation, oral coherence or CEFR attainment. A learner may choose different valid wording. Do not publish a model answer for the active task before the rehearsal. If real listening-to-speak is later introduced, use a fresh verified recording, keep transcript gated, and separate listening evidence from oral self-report.
