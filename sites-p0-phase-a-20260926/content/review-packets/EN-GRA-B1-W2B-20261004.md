## GRA-B1-06 — Second conditional: imaginary situations

**Module:** grammar  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 25 minutes

### Objectives

- Use if + past simple and would + base verb for imaginary or unlikely present/future situations.
- Keep would in the result clause rather than the if-clause in standard second-conditionals.
- Use the common hypothetical form If I were you to give advice.

### Explanation — English

Use the second conditional for imaginary, unlikely or hypothetical situations in the present or future. The usual pattern is if + past simple, would + base verb: If I had more time, I would learn another language. The past form does not mean past time here; it shows distance from reality. In standard second-conditionals, do not put would in the if-clause. The fixed advice pattern If I were you is especially common.

### Vietnamese support

Dùng câu điều kiện loại hai cho tình huống tưởng tượng, ít có khả năng hoặc giả định ở hiện tại/tương lai. Mẫu thường dùng là if + past simple, would + động từ nguyên mẫu. Dạng quá khứ ở đây không nói về thời gian quá khứ mà thể hiện khoảng cách với thực tế. Thông thường không dùng would trong mệnh đề if. Mẫu If I were you rất phổ biến khi đưa lời khuyên.

### Examples

- If I had more free time, I would join the art club.
- If she lived closer, we would see her more often.
- If it didn't rain so much, we would walk to school.
- If I were you, I would ask the teacher.

## GRA-B1-07 — Wish and if only for present situations

**Module:** grammar  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 24 minutes

### Objectives

- Use wish + past form to express that a present situation is different from what you want.
- Use if only for the same present-regret meaning with stronger emphasis.
- Use could after wish when the missing ability or possibility is the focus.

### Explanation — English

Use wish + a past form when you want a present situation to be different: I wish I knew the answer means I do not know it now. If only has a similar meaning but often sounds stronger: If only we lived closer! For ability or possibility, could is common: I wish I could play the guitar. These forms describe an unreal present, not a past event.

### Vietnamese support

Dùng wish + dạng quá khứ khi muốn một tình huống hiện tại khác đi: I wish I knew the answer nghĩa là hiện tại tôi không biết câu trả lời. If only có ý nghĩa tương tự nhưng thường nhấn mạnh hơn. Khi nói về khả năng hoặc điều có thể làm, thường dùng could: I wish I could play the guitar. Các cấu trúc này diễn tả hiện tại không như mong muốn, không phải sự việc quá khứ.

## GRA-B1-08 — Passive voice in the present and past

**Module:** grammar  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 25 minutes

### Objectives

- Form the present simple passive with am/is/are + past participle.
- Form the past simple passive with was/were + past participle.
- Choose the passive when the action or receiver is more important than the doer.

### Explanation — English

Use the passive when the action or the receiver is the main focus, or when the doer is unknown or unimportant. Form the present simple passive with am/is/are + past participle: Rice is grown in many countries. Form the past simple passive with was/were + past participle: The bridge was built in 1998. Add by + agent only when the doer is useful information.

Quá khứ đơn bị động: was/were + past participle. Chỉ thêm by + người thực hiện khi thông tin đó cần thiết.

## GRA-B1-09 — Reported statements and questions

**Module:** grammar  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 27 minutes

### Objectives

- Report basic statements after a past reporting verb using an appropriate backshift.
- Use statement word order in reported wh-questions.
- Use if or whether for basic reported yes/no questions.

### Explanation — English

Reported speech tells another person what someone said without repeating the exact words. After a past reporting verb, a present form often moves back: 'I am tired' → Mia said (that) she was tired; 'I need help' → Leo said that he needed help. Reported questions use statement word order, not direct-question order: 'Where do you live?' → She asked where I lived. For yes/no questions, use if or whether: 'Are you ready?' → He asked if I was ready. This lesson practices clear basic backshift examples where the earlier speech is being reported from a later past viewpoint.

### Vietnamese support

Reported speech dùng để kể lại lời người khác mà không lặp nguyên văn. Sau động từ tường thuật ở quá khứ, dạng hiện tại thường lùi thì: 'I am tired' → Mia said (that) she was tired. Câu hỏi tường thuật dùng trật tự câu kể, không dùng trật tự đảo của câu hỏi trực tiếp: 'Where do you live?' → She asked where I lived. Với câu hỏi yes/no, dùng if hoặc whether.

### Examples

- Mia said, 'I am tired.' → Mia said (that) she was tired.
- Leo said, 'I need help.' → Leo said that he needed help.
- She asked, 'Where do you live?' → She asked where I lived.
- He asked, 'Are you ready?' → He asked if I was ready.
