## REA-B1-04 — Why some study breaks work better

**Module:** reading  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 25 minutes

### Objectives

- Identify the writer's main claim and supporting contrast.
- Distinguish examples from the principle they illustrate.
- Infer why timing and break activity both affect concentration.
- Interpret cautious language in a balanced advice text.

### Explanation — English

Advice texts often combine a main principle with examples and limits. At B1, look for contrasts such as in contrast and while, and cautious phrases such as can, may and depends on. These show that the writer is not claiming one rule works for everyone.

### Vietnamese support

Văn bản lời khuyên thường có nguyên tắc chính, ví dụ và giới hạn. Hãy chú ý các từ nối tương phản như in contrast, while và các từ thận trọng như can, may, depends on.

### Examples

- MAIN CLAIM · A break should help the next work period.
- CONTRAST · Stretching creates a pause; a fast social feed may be hard to leave.
- LIMIT · The exact pattern is not identical for everyone.
- INFERENCE · Combine timing and activity to understand why a break helps or harms focus.

### Reading text

**Why some study breaks work better**

Short breaks can help during a long study session, but the effect depends on what a learner does before, during and after the break. A useful break gives attention a chance to reset without making it difficult to return to the task. Standing up, stretching, getting water or looking away from a screen for a few minutes can work well because these activities create a clear pause. In contrast, opening a fast-moving game or social feed may turn a five-minute break into twenty minutes and fill the learner's attention with new information. The timing also matters. Taking a break every few minutes can interrupt concentration before it becomes useful, while working for a very long period without stopping may lead to tired, careless work. A practical approach is to choose a reasonable work block, notice when attention is becoming less effective, and then take a short planned break. The exact pattern will not be identical for everyone. The important point is that a break should support the next period of focused work rather than become a new activity that is hard to leave.

## WRI-A2-04 — Reply to a message and solve a small problem

**Module:** writing  
**CEFR target:** A2  
**Estimated time:** 27 minutes

### Objectives

- Identify every practical point in a short message task.
- Organise a friendly reply that answers questions and proposes a solution.
- Use clear time/place language and a suitable closing.
- Write an independent response for AI or human review.

### Explanation — English

A useful A2 reply should answer the exact questions in the message. First underline the practical points: what changed, what the other person needs to know, and what solution you can offer. Then plan a simple order: greeting → answer/update → solution → final question or friendly closing. Keep times and places precise. Do not add private addresses or invented details that the task does not give you.

### Examples

- TASK · Answer the changed time and suggest a new meeting point.
- ORDER · Greeting → update → solution → closing.
- PRECISION · 'Meet at the library entrance at 4:15.'
- CHECK · Did I answer every practical question?

## WRI-B1-03 — Compare two options and recommend one

**Module:** writing  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 31 minutes

### Objectives

- Select relevant comparison criteria from a practical brief.
- Compare two options without inventing unsupported facts.
- Make a clear recommendation supported by evidence and a limitation.
- Use cohesive language and neutral register in an independent response.

### Explanation — English

A B1 recommendation should not simply list two options. Decide which criteria matter for the reader, compare the options using those criteria, then recommend one. Useful language includes both options…, whereas…, however…, because…, and therefore…. A strong response may acknowledge one disadvantage of the recommended option, but the final choice should remain clear and evidence-based.

### Vietnamese support

Bài gợi ý B1 cần chọn tiêu chí quan trọng, so sánh hai lựa chọn theo tiêu chí đó rồi đưa ra đề xuất rõ ràng. Có thể nêu một hạn chế của phương án được chọn, nhưng kết luận phải nhất quán và dựa trên dữ kiện.

### Examples

- CRITERIA · time, cost, location, learner needs
- COMPARE · Option A is closer, whereas Option B lasts longer.
- LIMIT · However, Option A has fewer places.
- RECOMMEND · Therefore, I would choose Option A because…
