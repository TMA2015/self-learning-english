## P01-U01 — My classroom: words for everyday things (Pre-A1, vocabulary)

**Prerequisite:** None; optional Vietnamese labels for new learners · **Time guide:** 12 minutes · **Status:** original draft, not live.

**1. Goal/context:** Recognise five common school words in short everyday contexts and match each word to its use; no CEFR level awarded.

**2. Teach the strategy:** Use a word together with a simple action. bag = carry books; door = enter or leave; chair = sit; desk = work or write; teacher = help learners in class. Say the words, then cover the bank before practice. Written text and optional Vietnamese support are available; pictures/audio are not required to answer.

**Optional Vietnamese support:** Ghép từ với hành động: bag = cặp, door = cửa ra vào, chair = ghế, desk = bàn học, teacher = giáo viên. Có thể xem từ gợi ý lúc đầu, sau đó che lại khi làm bài.

**Independent worked model:** Different model words (not scored): The board is at the front of the room. A window lets light in. Notice how each word is connected to a useful description.

**Steps:** 1. Read the short sentence and imagine the action. 2. Decide whether you need a person, place or object. 3. Try to remember the word before looking at the choices.

**3. Independent practice:** 5 new questions, reviewer-only keys and explanations below. The worked model is different from the practice situation.

**Optional hint (collapsed by default):** Is the sentence about carrying, closing, sitting, writing or helping learners?

**4. Feedback/remediation:** After Submit, reread the whole sentence, name the action and compare the meanings of the wrong options. Try one new sentence without looking at the word bank.

## P01-U02 — Can I have it, please? (Pre-A1, grammar_vocabulary)

**Prerequisite:** P01-U01 or equivalent classroom vocabulary · **Time guide:** 12 minutes · **Status:** original draft, not live.

**1. Goal/context:** Use a memorised polite request, response and thanks in a familiar classroom situation.

**2. Teach the strategy:** A simple polite request has a useful pattern: Can I have + thing + please? A person may reply Here you are. Then say Thank you. This is supported formula practice, not proof of independent conversation skill.

**Optional Vietnamese support:** Mẫu câu lịch sự: Can I have ... , please? = Cho mình xin ... được không? Here you are = Đây nhé. Thank you = Cảm ơn.

**Independent worked model:** Different model: Kim asks, 'Can I have the green crayon, please?' Jo gives it and says, 'Here you are.' Kim replies, 'Thank you!'

**Steps:** 1. Identify what you need. 2. Choose a short polite request rather than a command. 3. When someone helps, reply with thanks.

**3. Independent practice:** 5 new questions, reviewer-only keys and explanations below. The worked model is different from the practice situation.

**Optional hint (collapsed by default):** Look for Can I ... please? and a friendly reply.

**4. Feedback/remediation:** If you selected a command, compare how it sounds with a polite request. Repeat the three-part exchange using a different classroom object.

**New draft ID:** `DRAFT-P01-REA-A1-LOST-PROPERTY` — NOT a canonical existing lesson ID.

**Goal:** Understand a short everyday notice; find the location, deadline and required action; distinguish a stated rule from an assumption.

**Teach:** (1) First read the notice to find its purpose. (2) Read the question and classify it: main idea, place, time or action. (3) Use the complete sentence, particularly before/after/if; one familiar word is not sufficient. (4) For a question not directly stated, choose only what follows reasonably from the text. Highlighting is optional.

**Independent worked demonstration (NOT the assessed notice):**

*Mini-message:* “Chess Club meets on Friday at 3 p.m. Please bring a pen. New members may join this week.”

*Model thinking:* It is about a club meeting (purpose), on Friday at 3 p.m. (time), requiring a pen (action). “New members may join” suggests this is not only for members from the previous week (simple implication). Do not memorize this model for the next text.

**New assessed reading passage (original, unseen in worked demonstration):**

## Draft 2 — P01-U04 (A1 Vocabulary): What Do We Do at School?

**Status:** initial authored sample; needs independent CEFR and ambiguity review.  
**New draft ID:** `DRAFT-P01-VOC-A1-SCHOOL-ACTIONS`.

**Goal:** Understand useful action phrases; select collocations in context; retrieve a missing word without always seeing options.

**Teach:** A short natural expression is often easier to use than an isolated word. Notice the verb and what comes after it. Use `look for something` when trying to find it, `put away something` when returning it to its place, `take turns` when sharing an activity, and `sign up for an activity` when joining it. These are distinct classroom communication actions, not interchangeable synonyms.

**Worked model (different wording from independent questions):** A parent cannot find the house keys, so they **look for** them. After dinner, the family **put away** the dishes. For a board game, friends **take turns**. For a community event, volunteers **sign up** beforehand.

## P01-U05 — Write a short friendly invitation (A1, writing)

**2. Teach the strategy:** Before writing, check who will read the message and what they need to know. Use greeting → invitation → day/time → meeting place → friendly ending.
