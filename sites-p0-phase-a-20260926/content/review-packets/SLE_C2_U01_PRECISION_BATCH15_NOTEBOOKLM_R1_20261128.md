## 1. VOC-C2-02 — Diagnose lexical implications and calibrate commitment

**CORE-1 — Goal/context.** In unfamiliar editorial and professional contexts, choose between plausible near-equivalent formulations by tracking what each presupposes, what it commits the writer to, and what it foregrounds. Identify the point at which a collocation or idiom adds a meaning not licensed by the source. Explain choices in terms of the specific context, not supposed universal politeness rankings.

**CORE-2 — Teach/model.** Use a four-part contrast ledger: explicit proposition; background assumption; degree/scope of commitment; contextual interpersonal effect. Compare phrases in complete sentences, not in isolation. "Resume" normally presupposes an earlier activity that stopped; "initiate" does not. "Stop short of" describes a limit not crossed (often deliberately in an account of decisions); "fall short of" describes non-attainment of a target or expectation, not necessarily deliberate restraint. "In principle" can indicate agreement with a general idea before practical details are settled; an endorsement of an actual bounded plan is not automatically an endorsement of a wider principle. "Not least because" foregrounds an important reason without declaring it the sole or necessarily greatest reason. Conventional idioms have context-sensitive effects: "give someone the benefit of the doubt" means withhold an adverse conclusion despite uncertainty, not certify innocence or correctness. A word may carry an evaluative implication without proving a person's motive. In each case check the source status and identify exactly which implication would be changed by substitution.

Independent worked model (not assessed): a community archive paused indexing photographs while a consent form was revised. The committee now permits indexing again, but has not authorised public access. "The committee agreed to resume indexing" preserves the earlier pause and current permission. "The committee agreed to launch public access" changes both the action and its authority. "The committee fell short of authorising public access" might imply authorisation was a target it failed to attain; the record only says it was not authorised. A more neutral statement is "The committee authorised indexing to resume but made no decision on public access." In your ledger, identify which added implication is supported and which is not. Do not automatically prefer more elaborate language.

**CORE-3 — Independent practice (six fresh contexts).**

VOC-C2-02-Q1 — A conservation group's notes say public demonstrations were proposed for the first time this year. They approved preparation of two demonstrations but have not decided whether to open them to visitors. Which editorial sentence preserves the action and avoids an unsupported presupposition?
- A. The group has agreed to resume preparations for public demonstrations, with admission arrangements still unresolved. — "Resume" suggests earlier preparation that the notes do not establish.
- B. The group has agreed to begin preparing two demonstrations, with public access still undecided. — **Key B.** Preserves initiation, scope and decision status.
- C. The group has agreed to reinstate public demonstrations, subject to confirmation of the visitor timetable. — "Reinstate" suggests prior demonstrations and implies a decision on public access.
- D. The group has agreed in principle to reopen demonstrations to visitors, while preparation continues. — Adds an agreement about public access and presupposes earlier access.

VOC-C2-02-Q2 — The draft of a housing advice leaflet was reviewed solely for factual accuracy. The reviewer deliberately confined approval to the factual statements and explicitly left layout outside the review. Which account most accurately conveys that chosen boundary, rather than suggesting failure to attain a target?
- A. The review fell short of approving the leaflet's layout. — Can frame layout approval as an unmet goal, which was not the assigned review scope.
- B. The reviewer gave the leaflet an endorsement in principle, subject only to layout revisions. — Broad endorsement and required revisions are not established.
- C. The reviewer withheld factual approval pending a separate layout review. — Reverses the actual approval.
- D. The reviewer approved the factual statements but stopped short of endorsing the layout. — **Key D.** Expresses the explicit limit without treating layout approval as a failed review target.

VOC-C2-02-Q3 — A panel supports the general idea of a shared translation desk. It has not agreed to a specific pilot, staffing model or schedule. Which minute preserves this distinction between general agreement and operational endorsement?
- A. The panel endorsed the idea in principle, leaving any pilot and its arrangements to a later decision. — **Key A.** General support is distinct from approving a pilot.
- B. The panel gave qualified endorsement to the proposed pilot, leaving only the schedule to be confirmed. — Attributes pilot approval and narrows the unresolved details.
- C. The panel approved a pilot in outline, deferring the choice of staffing model. — Attributes operational approval not given.
- D. The panel agreed to implement the idea subject to finalising the arrangements. — Converts agreement in principle to an implementation commitment.

VOC-C2-02-Q4 — An editor says a public guide warrants revision for several reasons. She identifies the ambiguous appeal procedure as particularly important but expressly declines to rank it against other problems. Which formulation reflects that emphasis without introducing a ranking or exclusive cause?
- A. The guide warrants revision primarily because its appeal procedure is ambiguous. — Makes this the principal reason, contrary to the absence of ranking.
- B. The guide warrants revision insofar as its appeal procedure is ambiguous. — Restricts the claim to the extent of that particular ground rather than foregrounding it among several.
- C. The guide warrants revision, not least because its appeal procedure is ambiguous. — **Key C.** Highlights a substantial reason without imposing exclusivity or an explicit ranking.
- D. The guide warrants revision solely on account of its ambiguous appeal procedure. — Excludes the other stated reasons.

VOC-C2-02-Q5 — A volunteer's explanation of a delayed delivery is plausible but unverified. The coordinator decides not to assume misconduct, while still asking for the delivery record. Which account best conveys this particular interim stance?
- A. The coordinator took the explanation at face value and dispensed with further verification. — Treats the account as accepted and removes the requested check.
- B. The coordinator gave the volunteer the benefit of the doubt pending verification of the delivery record. — **Key B.** Withholds an adverse inference without certifying the explanation.
- C. The coordinator vouched for the volunteer's account while seeking supporting records. — Adds a personal assurance of the account's reliability.
- D. The coordinator provisionally found misconduct unsubstantiated following a completed inquiry. — Implies an inquiry and finding, rather than a decision not to prejudge.

VOC-C2-02-Q6 — A workshop organiser says "The new checklist is helpful insofar as it reminds volunteers to confirm equipment availability." The organiser has not examined whether it improves scheduling or safety. Which public paraphrase preserves both the positive evaluation and its scope?
- A. The organiser regards the checklist as helpful, not least because it improves scheduling and safety. — Adds effects and broadens the claim.
- B. The organiser regards the checklist as generally effective, albeit with room for improvement in safety. — Implies a general evaluation and an identified safety deficiency.
- C. The organiser endorses the checklist in principle as a comprehensive safety measure. — Attributes a different purpose and broader endorsement.
- D. The organiser identifies a specific benefit: the checklist prompts equipment checks; its effects on scheduling and safety remain unevaluated. — **Key D.** Retains the bounded evaluation without inferring other outcomes.

## 2. WRI-C2-01 — Reformulate precisely across audiences without losing pragmatic force

**CORE-1 — Goal/context.** Produce two concise original versions of a nuanced message: one for a collaborative working group, one for a formal decision record. Preserve evidential limits, conditional commitment, a speaker's actual criticism and an explicit authority boundary, while changing the linguistic realisation of interpersonal force. Explain the trade-offs of at least three wording choices. This is written practice, not an automatic C2 judgement.

**CORE-2 — Teach/model.** Plan Topic, Task, Must Include and Boundary. First make an invariant ledger (facts, extent of uncertainty, stance, proposed action, decision authority); then separate the intended effect for each audience. A working-group message may acknowledge a colleague's effort, invite repair and avoid attributing motive. A formal minute may foreground the exact defect, status and owner without conversational mitigation. Neither should erase a material objection, convert discussion into commitment, or imply blame without evidence. Avoid a mechanical direct/polite spectrum: explicitness can be considerate when it prevents mistaken action. Revise in three passes: proposition and presupposition; commitment and scope; audience and recoverable action. Independent miniature model (different topic): a neighbourhood garden team says the planting map is useful for locating beds but cannot establish whether a path meets accessibility requirements; only a facilities officer may clear the path. Working message: "The map gives us a useful starting point; could we have the path checked before describing it as accessible?" Formal minute: "The map identifies bed locations but does not establish path accessibility; clearance remains with the facilities officer." The first invites a check, the second records the boundary. Neither says the path is inaccessible, blames the mapmaker or claims clearance. Annotate an alternative: "The map proves accessibility" changes the evidential status; "the path is unsuitable" overstates the criticism. No answer model for the task below.
