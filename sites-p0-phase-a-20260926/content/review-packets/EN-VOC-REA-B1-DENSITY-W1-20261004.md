## VOC-B1-02 — Word families for study and communication

**Module:** vocabulary  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 23 minutes

### Objectives

- Recognize common noun, verb, adjective and adverb forms in familiar word families.
- Use suffix clues such as -tion, -ment, -ive and -ly to choose a form that fits a sentence.
- Use word-family knowledge together with sentence meaning rather than relying on spelling alone.

### Explanation — English

At B1, useful vocabulary growth includes noticing how one base can form several related words: decide → decision, improve → improvement, communicate → communication, effective → effectively. The correct form depends on the grammar and meaning of the sentence. A noun may follow an article, an adjective may describe a noun, and an adverb may describe how an action happens. Suffixes are helpful clues, but always check the whole context.

### Vietnamese support

Ở mức B1, học từ vựng hiệu quả bằng cách nhận ra các từ cùng họ: decide → decision, improve → improvement, communicate → communication, effective → effectively. Dạng đúng phụ thuộc vào ngữ pháp và nghĩa của cả câu. Hậu tố là gợi ý hữu ích nhưng phải luôn kiểm tra ngữ cảnh.

### Examples

- DECIDE → We made a difficult decision.
- IMPROVE → I can see a clear improvement.
- COMMUNICATE → Good communication helps a team.
- EFFECTIVE → The group worked effectively.

### Objectives

- Use common B1 collocations connected with plans, decisions and problem solving.

### Explanation — English

Fluent vocabulary use depends on combinations that commonly occur together. Useful B1 examples include make a decision, reach an agreement, solve a problem, meet a deadline, take responsibility, raise a concern and consider an option. ## REA-B1-02 — A repair café comes to school

**Module:** reading  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 24 minutes

### Objectives

- Identify the main purpose of an informative school text.
- Retrieve exact details about arrangements, roles and conditions.
- Infer meaning and vocabulary from surrounding evidence.

### Explanation — English

For a B1 information text, separate the overall purpose from exact details. Names, times, limits and conditions often answer detail questions directly. For inference and vocabulary-in-context, combine nearby clues rather than choosing an answer only because it sounds generally reasonable.

### Examples

### Reading text

Next Wednesday, Green Street Repair Café will run a two-hour session in our school library. The group normally meets once a month at the community centre, where volunteers help people repair small household objects instead of throwing them away. For this school visit, students may bring one broken item such as a desk lamp, a simple toy or a backpack with a damaged zip. Electrical items must be small enough to carry safely, and phones or tablets will not be accepted. The session begins at 3:30 p.m., but students with an item to repair should arrive at 3:15 so a volunteer can check it first. The organisers will also demonstrate how to sew on a button and replace a bicycle reflector. No previous repair experience is necessary. The aim is not simply to fix objects; the volunteers want students to understand how basic repairs can reduce waste and make useful items last longer. ## REA-B1-03 — Should study apps plan every session?

**Module:** reading  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 25 minutes

### Objectives

- Identify the writer's balanced main claim in a viewpoint text.
- Distinguish supporting details from qualifications and limitations.
- Infer the meaning and purpose of language used to qualify a claim.

### Explanation — English

A B1 viewpoint text may support an idea without presenting it as true in every situation. Look for balancing words such as however, can, may and depends on. Exact detail questions should be answered from the text, while inference questions require combining more than one clue.

### Vietnamese support

Văn bản nêu quan điểm B1 có thể ủng hộ một ý nhưng vẫn nêu giới hạn. Hãy chú ý các từ như however, can, may và depends on. Câu hỏi chi tiết cần bám sát văn bản; câu suy luận thường phải kết hợp nhiều dấu hiệu.

### Examples

- CLAIM · A tool can help without replacing learner decisions.
- QUALIFICATION · However, one plan does not suit every task.
- DETAIL · Find what the writer says the app can record.
- INFERENCE · Combine examples to understand the writer's attitude.

### Reading text

**Should study apps plan every session?**

Study apps can make it easier to organise homework. A learner can record a deadline, divide a large task into smaller steps and receive reminders before work is due. Some apps also show how much time has been spent on different subjects. These features can be useful, especially when several assignments have to be managed at once. However, an app cannot always decide what a learner should do next. A difficult maths problem may need more time than the original plan allowed, while a familiar vocabulary exercise may take less. Learners also differ: one person may work well with frequent reminders, while another may find them distracting. For this reason, a study plan should be treated as a guide rather than a set of fixed instructions. Checking progress is still valuable, but learners need to adjust the plan when the evidence changes. An app can support that process by making deadlines and study patterns visible. The final decision, though, still belongs to the learner.
