## Proposed learner-facing content (English)

### LIS-A2-01 — A workshop at the library
- Strategy / situation: Separate arrangements, requirements and what is supplied; mark each detail with its meaning.
- Independent worked example: Example text: The sewing session begins at seven in the evening. Bring some thread; fabric will be supplied. The charge is three euros.
- Reasoning / annotation: Seven is the start time, thread is the participant's responsibility, fabric is supplied. Three euros is a charge, not a time.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: Listen for verbs such as bring, supply and cost; do not put every mentioned object into the bring list.

### LIS-B1-01 — Choosing a reliable journey
- Strategy / situation: Distinguish a speaker's concession from the overall judgment and its condition.
- Independent worked example: Example text: The digital menu is convenient for many visitors, although some prefer a printed menu. The café should offer both formats when possible.
- Reasoning / annotation: Although acknowledges a limitation; the final recommendation is conditional. It is not a rejection of the digital menu.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: Replay around the contrast and conclusion; retain qualifiers instead of converting them to always or never.

### SPE-A2-01 — Talk about your routine
- Strategy / situation: Situation: describe a favourite weekend outing. Idea bank: place, one action, one feeling, one reason. Toolkit: On Saturdays…; then…; I enjoy… because…
- Independent worked example: Independent model: On Sundays, I visit the market with my uncle. Then we look at the flower stalls. I enjoy going there because the flowers are beautiful. We usually head home after that.
- Reasoning / annotation: Annotation: the time phrase establishes the situation; then connects actions; because explains a preference. Avoid a list of disconnected nouns.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: New practice: describe an afternoon at a community festival. Plan three keywords (place/action/reason), put the model away, speak for 20–40 seconds, and check whether the sequence and reason were clear. No required recording, grade or oral proficiency evidence.

### SPE-B1-01 — Explain and support an opinion
- Strategy / situation: Situation: explain a preference about a public space. Idea bank: purpose, benefits, trade-off, example. Toolkit: I'd prefer…; One reason is…; For instance…; On the other hand…
- Independent worked example: Independent model: I'd prefer more trees in the square. They provide shade, which makes the space comfortable. For instance, someone waiting for a friend could sit out of the sun. An open area might be better for a large event.
- Reasoning / annotation: Annotation: a concrete example supports the reason; the alternative is acknowledged without withdrawing the original preference. Avoid repeating the claim as its own reason.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: New practice: should a community hall have a mural or a display of local history? Plan position/reason/example/alternative in keywords; close the model, speak aloud, then answer when the other choice might be useful. Self-check coherence and fair qualification. No required recording, score or oral proficiency evidence.

### LIS-A1-01 — A school club announcement
- Strategy / situation: Find the event first; on a replay listen for one detail at a time.
- Independent worked example: Example text (not audio): The dance show is in Hall C on Sunday. It begins at half past six.
- Reasoning / annotation: The event is a dance show. Hall C is where, and half past six is when. Do not write a place in the time box.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: If you miss a detail, listen again for the relevant place or time word.

### LIS-A2-02 — A changed plan for Saturday
- Strategy / situation: Track the earlier plan, the revision and its reason in separate columns.
- Independent worked example: Example text: We planned to rehearse outside, but the ground is wet, so we'll practise in the gym instead. The rehearsal lasts an hour.
- Reasoning / annotation: Outside was the earlier plan. The wet ground explains the change; the gym is the updated place. The duration is separate.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: On replay, listen around but, so and instead; do not keep the old arrangement as the new one.

### LIS-B1-02 — Discussing a group-project problem
- Strategy / situation: Map the obstacle, suggested response and constraint; check the agreed action.
- Independent worked example: Example text: A: The costumes do not fit all performers. B: We can adjust the sleeves, but the fabric is limited. A: Let's alter only the ones that restrict movement. B: Agreed.
- Reasoning / annotation: The fabric constraint explains why they choose a limited alteration rather than altering every costume.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: If several responses are discussed, listen for acceptance and the condition that shapes it.

### LIS-A2-03 — Choosing a class at the sports centre
- Strategy / situation: Compare alternatives against the speaker's actual constraint; infer only from stated information.
- Independent worked example: Example text: A: Shall we order the large desk? B: It won't fit in the alcove. A: The narrow one has fewer drawers. B: That's fine; it will fit.
- Reasoning / annotation: The narrow desk meets the space requirement. More drawers are not decisive because the large desk will not fit.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: Track option, constraint and response; an attractive feature does not prove a final preference.

### LIS-B1-04 — Planning a student podcast episode
- Strategy / situation: Distinguish a preference, a compromise and the condition attached to it.
- Independent worked example: Example text: A: I'd prefer a full menu. B: We have one oven available. A: Then a smaller menu makes sense, provided we can include a vegetarian dish. B: We can do that.
- Reasoning / annotation: A prefers the full menu but accepts a smaller one if the vegetarian dish is included. This is conditional acceptance.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: Do not treat a preference as the final decision; listen for provided, if and the other person's response.

### LIS-A1-03 — The Bakery Order
- Strategy / situation: Keep the first request separate from the final choice.
- Independent worked example: Example text: A: A yellow balloon, please. B: We only have orange ones. A: I'll take an orange one.
- Reasoning / annotation: The yellow balloon was requested, but the last line changes the choice. The final item is orange.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: On replay, follow the words after the change rather than stopping at the first request.

### LIS-A1-04 — My New Neighbour
- Strategy / situation: Attach each detail to the person or thing it describes.
- Independent worked example: Example text: Leo likes chess. His cousin Sam likes singing. They often visit a pond.
- Reasoning / annotation: Chess belongs to Leo; singing belongs to Sam. A nearby name is not always the person described.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: On replay, make one small note per person; do not swap the two.

### LIS-A1-05 — The Missing Hat
- Strategy / situation: Listen for corrections, then match the complete description.
- Independent worked example: Example text: A: Is this your striped bag? B: No, mine has circles. A: Here is a bag with circles. B: Yes, that's mine.
- Reasoning / annotation: The first bag is rejected. Circles identify the bag in the final exchange; stripes do not.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: If two descriptions sound similar, replay the correction before deciding.

### LIS-A2-04 — Changing Trains After a Delay
- Strategy / situation: Distinguish an earlier arrangement from an alternative, and the reason from the action.
- Independent worked example: Example text: The outdoor film screening is unavailable because the projector has failed. We can watch a different film indoors, but we need to collect new passes first.
- Reasoning / annotation: The projector failure explains the change. The indoor film is an alternative; collecting passes is a step before it.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: Replay to link the action to the selected alternative, not to the unavailable plan.

### LIS-A2-05 — An Art Exhibition Reminder
- Strategy / situation: A reminder can revise one instruction while leaving other information in place.
- Independent worked example: Example text: For the craft demonstration, use the upstairs studio, not the courtyard. Your original reservation is still valid. Please switch off notifications during the talk.
- Reasoning / annotation: The location changes; the reservation does not. The last sentence is a request, not another location.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: Listen for not, still and please; mark change, unchanged condition and instruction separately.

### LIS-A2-06 — Repairing a Bicycle
- Strategy / situation: Distinguish a possibility from a promise, and a suggestion from an accepted plan.
- Independent worked example: Example text: A: The tailor may finish your jacket this evening. B: I need it for a ceremony. A: You could wear the plain coat. B: Yes, I'll do that.
- Reasoning / annotation: May is uncertain, not a promise. The plain coat is first a suggestion, then accepted by the final response.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: Replay the response to each suggestion; do not turn may into will.

### LIS-B1-05 — A Library of Things
- Strategy / situation: Follow how a speaker's position develops; identify the reason for a change rather than guessing a feeling.
- Independent worked example: Example text: A: I'm doubtful about the new noticeboard. B: It will include a large-print section. A: That addresses my main concern. I'd support a trial.
- Reasoning / annotation: The speaker moves from doubt to support for a trial because the concern is addressed. The speaker has not endorsed permanent adoption.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: Compare the initial and final statements; preserve the scope of the final commitment.

### LIS-B1-06 — What We Learned from a Pop-Up Sale
- Strategy / situation: Separate what happened from the speaker's later interpretation and next action.
- Independent worked example: Example text: Our choir's first rehearsal sounded uneven. We changed the seating, and the voices blended better. I realised that the arrangement mattered more than singing louder. Next week, we'll test two layouts.
- Reasoning / annotation: Changing seats is the action; better blending is the observed result. The final insight concerns arrangement, and the next test is still planned.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: Use separate notes for event, result, interpretation and future plan; do not turn the insight into an observed fact.

### LIS-B1-07 — Checking a Photo Before Sharing
- Strategy / situation: Separate direct observations, possible interpretations and evidence limits.
- Independent worked example: Example text: A: The attendance register shows fewer visitors this month. B: Perhaps the new timetable matters, but we haven't asked them. A: We should collect responses before changing the schedule.
- Reasoning / annotation: Fewer visits is an observation; the timetable is a possible explanation, not established causation. Responses are proposed as additional evidence.
- Pitfall repair / independent practice: If a claim sounds confident, check whether the speaker actually provides support; preserve perhaps and other limits.
