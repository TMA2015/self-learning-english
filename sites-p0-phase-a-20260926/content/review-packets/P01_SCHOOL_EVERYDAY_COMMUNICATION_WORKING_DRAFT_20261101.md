## Draft 1 — P01-U03 (A1 Reading): The Lost-Property Shelf

**Status:** first-pass original content; needs independent academic/answer review.  
**New draft ID:** `DRAFT-P01-REA-A1-LOST-PROPERTY` — NOT a canonical existing lesson ID.

**Goal:** Understand a short everyday notice; find the location, deadline and required action; distinguish a stated rule from an assumption.

**Teach:** (1) First read the notice to find its purpose. (2) Read the question and classify it: main idea, place, time or action. (3) Use the complete sentence, particularly before/after/if; one familiar word is not sufficient. (4) For a question not directly stated, choose only what follows reasonably from the text. Highlighting is optional.

**Independent worked demonstration (NOT the assessed notice):**

*Mini-message:* “Chess Club meets on Friday at 3 p.m. Please bring a pen. New members may join this week.”

*Model thinking:* It is about a club meeting (purpose), on Friday at 3 p.m. (time), requiring a pen (action). “New members may join” suggests this is not only for members from the previous week (simple implication). Do not memorize this model for the next text.

**New assessed reading passage (original, unseen in worked demonstration):**

> LOST PROPERTY — Your school office has a shelf for things that students have left behind. This week, staff found a red lunch box, a blue water bottle and a small green scarf. The shelf is beside the main office door, not inside the library. If you think an item belongs to you, tell a member of staff what it looks like. Collect your item before Thursday at 4 p.m. On Friday morning, items that remain will be moved to the school storage room. You do not need to pay to collect your own item.

**Independent questions (new draft IDs, answer keys and why):**

| P01-U03-Q2 | Where is the shelf? | A: Beside the main office door; B: In the library; C: In the storage room | A | The notice directly contrasts the office entrance with the library |

## Draft 2 — P01-U04 (A1 Vocabulary): What Do We Do at School?

**Status:** initial authored sample; needs independent CEFR and ambiguity review.  
**New draft ID:** `DRAFT-P01-VOC-A1-SCHOOL-ACTIONS`.

**Goal:** Understand useful action phrases; select collocations in context; retrieve a missing word without always seeing options.

**Teach:** A short natural expression is often easier to use than an isolated word. Notice the verb and what comes after it. Use `look for something` when trying to find it, `put away something` when returning it to its place, `take turns` when sharing an activity, and `sign up for an activity` when joining it. These are distinct classroom communication actions, not interchangeable synonyms.

**Worked model (different wording from independent questions):** A parent cannot find the house keys, so they **look for** them. After dinner, the family **put away** the dishes. For a board game, friends **take turns**. For a community event, volunteers **sign up** beforehand.

**Independent objective practice:**

| ID | Prompt | Expected answer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P01-U04-Q1 | Two students want to use one microscope. They should... A: take turns; B: look for it; C: sign up for it | take turns | Sharing one resource means each person gets a turn |
| P01-U04-Q2 | After art class, pupils should ___ the paint brushes. A: put away; B: take turns; C: look for | put away | The sentence requires a verb phrase meaning return the brushes to their proper place |
| P01-U04-Q3 | Mina cannot see her notebook. She will ___ it before the next lesson. (enter missing words) | look for | She is trying to find a missing object |
| P01-U04-Q4 | We want to join the lunchtime music group. We must ___ for it online. (TWO missing words) | sign up | Sign up for means register for an organised activity; the answer contains two words |
| P01-U04-Q5 | Three pupils share one tablet. Which phrase describes using it one after another? A: take turns; B: put away; C: sign up | take turns | Take turns describes fair sequential use; the other phrases mean store or register |

**Editorial repair completed 2026-11-02:** Q2 syntax, Q4 two-word blank and Q5 complete options were corrected. Independent reviewer must still check all distractors and acceptable short-answer variants. Free-form meaning is not automatically graded.

**Optional review:** One day later, show the action scenario without multiple-choice options. Scheduling is a recommendation, not a promise of memory retention.
