## 1. GRA-C1-02 — Control the scope of qualification and conditions
**CORE-1 — Goal/context.** Edit a formal evaluation so readers distinguish what is uncertain, what is established, and which conditions apply to which recommendation. Choose language that preserves the logical scope of negation, modality, necessity and sufficiency. This is more than substituting a weaker modal.
**CORE-2 — Teach the concept.** A qualifier applies to a particular claim or component. “It is not clear that X happened” leaves X unsettled; “It is clear that X did not happen” asserts non-occurrence. “Not necessarily X” denies inevitability, not the possibility of X. “Only if P, Q” makes P necessary for Q, not sufficient. “Provided that P, Q” states a condition for Q in the given context; it does not by itself say that P is the sole condition or that P has been met. “Even if P, Q” presents Q as holding despite P in the stated hypothetical; do not mistake concession for a factual assertion that P is true.
| Intended distinction | Wording in an independent scenario | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Uncertain positive claim vs confident negative | It is not established that the seedlings survived the frost. / It is established that they did not survive. | Evidence gap vs established negative. |
| Not inevitable vs impossible | A change in container size need not improve growth. | Improvement is not guaranteed; may still happen. |
| Necessary but insufficient | The garden can reopen only if the path is repaired. | Repair is required; other conditions may remain. |
| Condition vs concession | If the supplier confirms delivery, we can plan the session. / Even if delivery is confirmed, we must still check staffing. | The second obligation remains in the hypothetical. |
Worked model: A seed-library team has permission to advertise an event, but access and staff availability are unresolved. “Permission to advertise is necessary for a public announcement, but it is not sufficient to establish that the event can take place.” The first clause identifies a requirement, the second prevents a stronger inference. “It is not yet clear that enough staff will be available” does not assert that there will be a shortage. To edit, identify the proposition affected by the modal/negative, draw the condition arrow, and keep other stated constraints.
**CORE-3 — Independent practice.** Choose the best wording or interpretation that preserves the intended scope in context.
GRA-C1-02-Q1 — The monitoring team cannot establish whether the new filter reduced the concentration of a substance. A report must preserve uncertainty rather than assert failure. Which sentence is most faithful?
- A. It is established that the new filter did not reduce the concentration. — Asserts a negative outcome.
- B. It has not been established that the new filter reduced the concentration. — **Key B.** Negates establishment of a positive claim, leaving the outcome open.
- C. It has been established that the new filter had no measurable effect. — Introduces a verified finding.
- D. The team has established that the reduction was too small to justify the filter. — Invents magnitude and decision.

GRA-C1-02-Q2 — A committee says a larger venue will be considered only if step-free access can be assured. The budget and staffing still require approval. Which inference follows?
- A. Assured step-free access is a necessary condition for consideration, but it does not guarantee approval. — **Key A.** Correctly preserves necessary condition and unresolved requirements.
- B. Once access is assured, the committee must approve the venue. — Converts a requirement for consideration into a sufficient condition for approval.
- C. The committee has already approved a venue with step-free access. — Treats conditional consideration as an actual decision.
- D. The committee will not consider any accessible venue unless every other proposal has failed. — Invents an additional condition.

GRA-C1-02-Q3 — A reviewer writes, “A shorter application process need not result in a more representative set of applicants.” Which interpretation preserves the modal scope?
- A. A shorter process cannot yield a more representative applicant group. — Converts lack of necessity into impossibility.
- B. A shorter process is unnecessary whenever the group is representative. — Reverses the relationship and changes scope.
- C. A shorter process does not guarantee a more representative group, although it might help. — **Key C.** Denies the necessary result without ruling it out.
- D. A more representative group proves that the application process was shorter. — Reverses the direction of inference.

GRA-C1-02-Q4 — The proposed equipment loan depends on funding; safety training remains mandatory whether or not funding is confirmed. Which formulation keeps the second requirement independent?
- A. Unless funding is confirmed, safety training will no longer be needed. — Makes the requirement contingent on funding.
- B. Provided that funding is confirmed, safety training is the only remaining requirement. — Adds an exclusive condition not supplied.
- C. Safety training will be mandatory only if funding is confirmed. — Restricts the training obligation to the funded case.
- D. Even if funding is confirmed, safety training remains mandatory. — **Key D.** Preserves the obligation despite the hypothetical confirmation.

GRA-C1-02-Q5 — A report records that only one of several explanations has been tested, and that test did not settle the issue. Which wording keeps the quantifier and evidential limitation accurate?
- A. Not all of the proposed explanations have been tested, and the available test is inconclusive. — **Key A.** Avoids asserting that none were tested or that the test disproved an explanation.
- B. None of the proposed explanations has been tested conclusively, so no test has taken place. — Incorrectly infers absence of testing.
- C. All of the proposed explanations have been tested but none has been supported. — Changes both the test scope and result.
- D. The untested explanations must be more plausible than the tested one. — Unsupported comparison.

GRA-C1-02-Q6 — A team is confident that a report omitted two entries, but it is uncertain whether the omission affected the final estimate. Which sentence attaches uncertainty to the right proposition?
- A. The report may have omitted two entries, and the omission affected the estimate. — Weakens the known fact and asserts the unknown effect.
- B. Although the report omitted two entries, it is unclear whether that omission affected the estimate. — **Key B.** Preserves established omission and uncertain consequence.
- C. The report apparently omitted two entries because the estimate was inaccurate. — Adds a causal explanation and implies an unverified inaccuracy.
- D. Since the effect is uncertain, it is also uncertain whether any entries were omitted. — Transfers uncertainty to an established fact.

**CORE-4 — Feedback and repair.** Use the post-submit rationales. Q1/Q3/Q6: underline the exact proposition affected by negation or modality. Q2/Q4: express the condition in words before choosing a clause. Q5: distinguish “not all” from “none” and “inconclusive” from “not attempted”. Draft two sentences preserving the known count, uncertainty and necessary condition. Explain which proposition your qualifier governs and why renewal is not guaranteed. ## 2. GRA-C1-03 — Choose marked emphasis without distorting information
**CORE-1 — Goal/context.** Decide when a marked construction helps foreground a contrast, restrict a condition or organise a formal argument, and when neutral wording is clearer. Distinguish negative/restrictive inversion, conditional inversion and cleft focus; preserve meaning and register rather than using complexity for prestige.
**CORE-2 — Teach the concept.** In formal English, fronted negative or restrictive adverbials often trigger subject–auxiliary inversion in the relevant main clause: “Never had the team faced such a delay”; “Only after the review did the board agree.” If no suitable auxiliary exists, use do-support. “Only” modifying the subject does not trigger this pattern: “Only the board approved the change.” With “only after” introducing a subordinate clause, the main clause inverts, not the subordinate clause. Conditional inversion can replace “if” in specific formal patterns: “Had the team known…” (unreal past), “Should further evidence emerge…” (possible future condition), “Were the timetable to change…” (hypothetical). These are not interchangeable; they preserve distinct hypothetical relations. Clefts already introduced in GRA-C1-01 foreground an element; they do not justify inventing a contrast. Negative fronting can introduce stronger or more sweeping emphasis than the source warrants. Use neutral wording where marked emphasis would mislead or be needlessly formal.
| Independent example | What the form foregrounds |
|---|---|
| Only after the rehearsal ended did the director explain the change. | Timing restriction; inversion in the main clause. |
| Only the director explained the change. | Restriction on subject; no inversion. |
| Had the rehearsal been recorded, the director could have reviewed the timing. | Unreal past; possible result, not guaranteed. |
| Should another rehearsal be needed, contact the director. | Formal possible condition. |
| The director explained the change after the rehearsal. | Neutral chronology, no claim that earlier explanation was impossible. |
Worked model: A theatre report states that the stage manager, not the ticket team, checked the venue access; the check took place after an initial visit. “It was the stage manager who checked venue access” focuses on the agent, without adding a claim about timing. “Only after the initial visit did the stage manager check access” implies the check did not occur earlier; use that only if the facts warrant the restriction. Choose a construction from the intended contrast, not from a desire to sound advanced.
**CORE-3 — Independent practice.**
GRA-C1-03-Q1 — The record explicitly states that the panel withheld its decision until a revised safety assessment arrived. Which formal sentence foregrounds that temporal restriction and is standard English?
- A. Only after did the revised assessment arrive the panel make its decision. — Misplaces inversion and disrupts subordinate-clause structure.
- B. Only the revised assessment did arrive before the panel made its decision. — Changes focus from timing restriction to the assessment.
- C. Only after the revised safety assessment arrived did the panel make its decision. — **Key C.** Correct main-clause inversion and warranted timing restriction.
- D. Only after the revised assessment arrived the panel did make its decision. — Wrong word order for fronted restrictive adverbial.

GRA-C1-03-Q2 — “If the organisers had consulted the transport team earlier, they might have identified the problem before the announcement.” Which formal alternative retains the counterfactual and uncertainty?
- A. Had the organisers consulted the transport team earlier, they might have identified the problem before the announcement. — **Key A.** Inverted unreal-past conditional; retains might.
- B. Should the organisers consult the transport team earlier, they will identify the problem before the announcement. — Shifts to prospective condition and stronger result.
- C. Had the organisers consulted the team earlier, they would certainly have identified the problem. — Adds certainty absent from the original.
- D. Were the organisers to consult the team now, the earlier announcement would have been corrected. — Mixes present hypothesis with an unsupported past outcome.

GRA-C1-03-Q3 — In a neutral factual update, only one named committee is authorised to change the schedule; no special timing condition exists. Which formulation preserves this subject restriction without adding a condition?
- A. Only after the committee meets can the schedule be changed. — Introduces a timing condition.
- B. Only the scheduling committee is authorised to change the schedule. — **Key B.** Restricts the subject, not the time.
- C. Only is the scheduling committee authorised to change the schedule. — Incorrect inversion after subject restriction.
- D. The scheduling committee is authorised to change only the schedule. — Restricts what may be changed rather than who may change it.

GRA-C1-03-Q4 — A policy note says, “If further objections arise, the facilitator will arrange another discussion.” The writer wants a formal conditional inversion without changing the commitment. Which version works?
- A. Had further objections arisen, the facilitator would have arranged another discussion. — Shifts to an unreal past.
- B. Should further objections arise, the facilitator will arrange another discussion. — **Key B.** Preserves possible future condition and stated commitment.
- C. Were further objections to arise, the facilitator might have arranged a previous discussion. — Changes time and commitment.
- D. Should the facilitator arrange another discussion, further objections will arise. — Reverses the conditional relation.

GRA-C1-03-Q5 — The record says the access route was altered on Monday. It does not establish whether earlier alterations occurred. Which sentence is suitable for a neutral report?
- A. Not until Monday was the access route altered. — Adds a claim of no earlier alteration.
- B. Never before Monday had the access route been altered. — Adds an even stronger unsupported claim.
- C. Only on Monday did the team alter the access route. — Restricts the timing beyond the record.
- D. The access route was altered on Monday. — **Key D.** Reports the documented event without adding a temporal restriction.

GRA-C1-03-Q6 — A reviewer wants to contrast what requires revision: the method of selecting records, not the format of the final report. Which sentence foregrounds the intended contrast without implying a new finding?
- A. It is the method of selecting records, rather than the format of the final report, that requires revision. — **Key A.** Cleft highlights exactly the intended element; useful bridge from GRA-C1-01.
- B. Only after the format is revised can the method be examined. — Adds a procedural requirement.
- C. What requires revision is the format, regardless of the selection method. — Reverses the focus.
- D. Not only has the method been revised, but the format has also been replaced. — Reports completed actions and changes the meaning.

**CORE-4 — Feedback and repair.** Q1/Q3: identify whether the restrictive expression is the subject or a fronted adjunct; place inversion only where licensed. Q2/Q4: reconstruct the original if-clause and compare time and modality. Q5/Q6: check what the source actually contrasts or excludes; prefer the neutral form if restriction is not warranted. Independent unassessed transfer: a fictional workshop could have avoided a timetable clash had it checked the booking list; a future enquiry should be referred to the venue coordinator if one arises. Write a formal conditional inversion for each. Then choose a neutral or emphatic wording for a documented cancellation on Wednesday where earlier events are unknown. Explain why a marked restrictive form might add unsupported meaning. No auto-score.

## 3. GRA-C1-04 — Package information without concealing agency or scope
**CORE-1 — Goal/context.** Compare compact noun phrases and full clauses in formal reports. Choose nominalisation, premodification, postmodification or a finite clause to preserve agency, evidential status, attachment and time. Compactness is useful only when the reader can recover the intended relations.
**CORE-2 — Teach the concept.** Nominalisation packages a process as a noun (“review” / “the review of the proposal”). It can provide a cohesive topic for the next sentence, but may obscure who acted, whether an action actually occurred, or how a modifier attaches. A noun phrase may be concise without being more precise. “The review of the proposal by the committee” typically identifies the reviewer; when multiple modifiers make attachment unclear, use a full clause. Distinguish a proposed event from an event already completed: “the proposed withdrawal” and “the withdrawal” can imply different status. A passive may appropriately foreground the affected entity; retain a named agent when attribution matters. Be particularly careful with stacked nouns and nominalisations of uncertain claims; do not treat a hypothesis as a known event.
| Independent example | Choice and reason |
|---|---|
| The conservator assessed the damage. This assessment informed the repair plan. | Nominalisation links known information to the next point. |
| The proposed relocation of the paintings is under discussion. | Proposed, not completed. |
| The conservator examined the frame of a painting that had been damaged. | Attachment can be unclear; state what was damaged explicitly. |
| A librarian reported that volunteers might have mislabelled two books. | Attribution and uncertainty remain clear. |
Worked model: “The committee's rejection of the exhibition proposal” is unsuitable if a committee has only expressed reservations; it turns a possible decision into an established rejection. Prefer “The committee expressed reservations about the proposal.” If the rejection is documented and the next sentence discusses its consequences, the nominalisation can be an effective cohesive topic. When editing, ask: what happened or is proposed; who acted or reported; what modifies what; which claim is uncertain; does the reader need the time or agent?
**CORE-3 — Independent practice.**
GRA-C1-04-Q1 — An independent auditor reported that a contractor might have omitted a required inspection. The omission is not established. Which revision preserves attribution and uncertainty?
- A. The contractor's omission of a required inspection was confirmed by the auditor. — Turns a reported possibility into confirmation.
- B. The auditor's confirmation of the contractor's omission prompted the enquiry. — Adds confirmation and consequence.
- C. An independent auditor reported a possible omission of a required inspection by the contractor. — **Key C.** Retains source, possible status and relevant agent.
- D. The required inspection's omission proves that the auditor's report was inaccurate. — Adds an unrelated conclusion.

GRA-C1-04-Q2 — A report must make clear that the school, not the supplier, rejected the proposed delivery dates. Which alternative is least ambiguous?
- A. The rejection of the school's delivery dates proposal was recorded. — Does not name the rejecting agent and may misattribute the proposal.
- B. The school rejected the delivery dates proposed by the supplier. — **Key B.** Finite clause preserves both agent and proposal ownership.
- C. The supplier's rejection of the school's proposed dates was recorded. — Reverses both roles.
- D. The school supplier delivery date rejection was noted. — Stacked modifiers obscure the relationship.

GRA-C1-04-Q3 — The steering group has discussed withdrawing a pilot scheme, but no decision has been made. Which sentence accurately introduces this topic for a subsequent paragraph?
- A. The withdrawal of the pilot scheme has altered the group's priorities. — Implies a completed withdrawal and adds a consequence.
- B. The group's decision to withdraw the pilot requires implementation. — Invents decision.
- C. The withdrawn pilot will need a replacement. — Treats withdrawal as fact.
- D. The possible withdrawal of the pilot scheme remains under discussion. — **Key D.** Preserves the unresolved status.

GRA-C1-04-Q4 — “The team revised the survey wording. This change did not address the lack of responses from non-users.” Which concise version best preserves both the actor and the limitation?
- A. The team's revision of the survey wording did not address the lack of responses from non-users. — **Key A.** Nominalisation packages the known action without changing attribution or scope.
- B. The revision of non-users' responses corrected the survey wording. — Changes the object and outcome.
- C. The team's revision resolved the problem of missing responses. — Reverses the limitation.
- D. The team's revised survey proved that non-users had no concerns. — Invents a finding.

GRA-C1-04-Q5 — The phrase “the review of the applications from the committee” is ambiguous in a context where the committee reviewed applications submitted by students. Which revision explicitly resolves the relevant attachment?
- A. The committee's applications underwent a review. — Treats the committee as applicant.
- B. The committee reviewed the applications submitted by students. — **Key B.** Makes reviewer and submitters explicit.
- C. The review by applicants from the committee was completed. — Reassigns the reviewer.
- D. The student review committee applications were considered. — Retains ambiguous stacked nouns.

GRA-C1-04-Q6 — Last year's data were analysed this month; the results have not yet been checked. Which report sentence preserves both time reference and the limited status of the findings?
- A. Last year's analysis confirmed the results this month. — Locates analysis in the wrong year and invents confirmation.
- B. This month's data analysis of last year's results has verified the findings. — Changes the objects and adds verification.
- C. The team analysed last year's data this month; the results still await verification. — **Key C.** Distinguishes data period, analysis time and verification status.
- D. The verified analysis of this month's data was completed last year. — Reverses time and status.

**CORE-4 — Feedback and repair.** Q1/Q2/Q5: map who acted, who reported and what each modifier describes. Q3: mark proposed vs decided vs completed. Q4/Q6: retain the relevant agent, time and limitation even if a longer clause is clearer. Independent unassessed transfer: a fictional archive report says a volunteer suggested removing duplicate labels; the manager has not approved removal; a separate review examined last year's entries this week. Draft a concise two-sentence update. Use a nominalisation only if it preserves status and agency; explain one place a finite clause is clearer. No auto-score.
