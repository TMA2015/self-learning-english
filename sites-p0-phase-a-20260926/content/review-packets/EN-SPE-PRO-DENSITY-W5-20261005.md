## SPE-A1-01 — Introduce yourself and ask a simple question

**Module:** speaking  
**CEFR target:** A1  
**Estimated time:** 14 minutes

### Objectives

- Give a short introduction using basic personal information chosen by the learner.
- Use a simple greeting and closing.
- Ask one clear everyday question.
- Speak from keywords instead of reading a full script.

### Explanation — English

A simple introduction can be short: greeting, your preferred name, one safe detail such as a hobby or favourite school subject, then one question for the other person. You do not need to share private information such as your address, phone number or full legal name. Plan a few keywords and speak naturally.

### Vietnamese support

Một phần giới thiệu đơn giản có thể rất ngắn: chào hỏi, tên muốn được gọi, một thông tin an toàn như sở thích hoặc môn học yêu thích, rồi hỏi người kia một câu. Không cần chia sẻ địa chỉ, số điện thoại hay họ tên đầy đủ. Chỉ ghi vài từ khóa rồi nói tự nhiên.

### Examples

- GREETING · Hi! I'm Linh.
- SAFE DETAIL · I like drawing.
- QUESTION · What do you like doing after school?
- CLOSING · Nice to meet you.

## SPE-B1-03 — Explain a problem and propose a practical solution

**Module:** speaking  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 19 minutes

### Objectives

- Describe a practical problem clearly.
- Explain why the problem matters.
- Propose a realistic solution and support it with a reason.
- Respond to one concern or follow-up question.

### Explanation — English

For a B1 problem-solution response, make the situation easy to follow: state the problem, explain its effect, suggest a practical solution and give a reason. Then respond directly to one possible concern. You do not need a long speech; clear organisation and specific support matter more.

### Vietnamese support

- EFFECT · It is difficult to concentrate.
- SOLUTION · We could create one quiet table near the back.

## PRO-A2-03 — Hear the three common -ed endings

**Module:** pronunciation  
**CEFR target:** A2  
**Estimated time:** 16 minutes

### Objectives

- Recognise the common /t/, /d/ and /ɪd/ pronunciations of regular past-tense -ed.
- Match familiar regular verbs to the correct ending pattern.
- Practise the ending after listening to a real audio model.

### Explanation — English

Regular past-tense -ed is not always pronounced as a separate syllable. In common standard pronunciation, it can sound like /t/ as in worked, /d/ as in played, or /ɪd/ as in wanted. The spelling stays -ed, but the sound changes according to the final sound of the base verb. Listen to a model before practising.

### Vietnamese support

Đuôi -ed của động từ quá khứ có ba cách phát âm phổ biến: /t/ như worked, /d/ như played, và /ɪd/ như wanted. Cách viết vẫn là -ed nhưng âm thay đổi theo âm cuối của động từ gốc. Hãy nghe âm thanh mẫu trước khi luyện.

### Examples

- /t/ · worked, watched
- /d/ · played, cleaned
- /ɪd/ · wanted, needed

## PRO-B1-02 — Use intonation to ask, check and confirm

**Module:** pronunciation  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 17 minutes

### Objectives

- Notice common rising and falling intonation patterns in short questions.
- Distinguish a normal information question from a checking or confirming question.
- Use intonation to make a short spoken question easier to interpret.

### Explanation — English

Intonation is the movement of the voice across a phrase. Many ordinary wh- questions use a falling pattern, while yes/no questions often rise. A speaker can also use a rise to check or confirm information. These are common patterns, not rigid rules: context and speaker intention matter. Listen to the full model before copying the pattern.

### Vietnamese support

Ngữ điệu là sự lên xuống của giọng trong một cụm nói. Nhiều câu hỏi wh- thông thường có xu hướng xuống giọng, còn câu hỏi yes/no thường lên giọng. Người nói cũng có thể lên giọng để kiểm tra hoặc xác nhận thông tin. Đây là xu hướng phổ biến chứ không phải luật tuyệt đối; ngữ cảnh và ý định người nói rất quan trọng.

### Examples

- FALLING · Where is the meeting room?
- RISING · Is the meeting at three?
- CHECKING · Room fourteen?
- CONTEXT · The same words can sound different when the speaker's intention changes.
