# LESSON 1 — VOC-A2-05

**Module:** vocabulary  
**CEFR target:** A2  
**Title:** Food preparation and simple instructions  
**Estimated time:** 20 minutes  
**Activities:** 8

## Objectives

Learners will:
1. understand common food-preparation verbs in everyday instructions;
2. choose natural verb–noun combinations;
3. use context to distinguish similar actions such as chop, mix, pour, heat and serve.

## Learn

Key chunks:

- **chop vegetables** — cut them into smaller pieces;
- **mix ingredients** — combine them together;
- **pour water / milk** — make liquid flow from one container to another;
- **heat soup** — make it hot;
- **add salt / sugar** — put an extra ingredient in;
- **serve food** — give prepared food to people to eat.

Useful nouns:
- **ingredient** — one food item used to make a dish;
- **recipe** — instructions explaining how to prepare a dish.

Vietnamese micro-support:
- ingredient = nguyên liệu;
- recipe = công thức;
- chop = cắt nhỏ;
- pour = rót/đổ;
- serve = dọn/phục vụ món ăn.

## Activities

### VOC-A2-05-Q1
**Type:** single_choice  
**Prompt:** You want smaller pieces of carrot for a salad. What should you do?  
A. chop the carrot  
B. pour the carrot  
C. serve the carrot  
D. heat the carrot  
**Answer:** A  
**Why:** *Chop* means cut food into smaller pieces. *Pour* is for liquids.

### VOC-A2-05-Q2
**Prompt:** Complete naturally: “___ the flour and eggs in the bowl.”  
A. Mix  
B. Pour  
C. Serve  
D. Chop  
**Answer:** A  
**Why:** Flour and eggs can be combined by mixing them.

### VOC-A2-05-Q3
**Prompt:** Which instruction is natural?  
A. Pour the water into the cup.  
B. Chop the water into the cup.  
C. Serve the water into the cup.  
D. Mix the cup into the water.  
**Answer:** A  
**Why:** *Pour* is used when moving liquid from one container to another.

### VOC-A2-05-Q4
**Prompt:** The soup is cold. Which instruction best fits?  
A. Heat the soup before dinner.  
B. Chop the soup before dinner.  
C. Pour the soup before dinner.  
D. Mix the dinner before soup.  
**Answer:** A  
**Why:** *Heat* means make something warmer or hot.

### VOC-A2-05-Q5
**Prompt:** A recipe says, “Add one teaspoon of sugar.” What does *add* mean here?  
A. put in an extra ingredient  
B. remove an ingredient  
C. cut the ingredient into pieces  
D. give the finished food to guests  
**Answer:** A  
**Why:** In a recipe, *add* means put another ingredient into the mixture.

### VOC-A2-05-Q6
**Prompt:** Which sentence uses *serve* correctly?  
A. We served the rice with vegetables.  
B. We served the onion into tiny pieces.  
C. We served the milk into the glass.  
D. We served the soup until it became hot.  
**Answer:** A  
**Why:** *Serve* is used for giving prepared food to people.

### VOC-A2-05-Q7
**Prompt:** Which word means “one of the foods used to make a dish”?  
A. ingredient  
B. receipt  
C. timetable  
D. journey  
**Answer:** A  
**Why:** An ingredient is one item used in preparing food.

### VOC-A2-05-Q8
**Prompt:** You have never made vegetable soup before. Which item would most directly tell you the steps?  
A. a recipe  
B. a receipt  
C. a ticket  
D. a timetable  
**Answer:** A  
**Why:** A recipe gives instructions for preparing a dish.

# LESSON 2 — VOC-B1-05

**Module:** vocabulary  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Title:** Learning goals, progress and feedback  
**Estimated time:** 22 minutes  
**Activities:** 8

## Objectives

Learners will:
1. understand common B1 collocations used to talk about learning and improvement;
2. distinguish natural combinations from grammatically possible but unnatural ones;
3. infer the meaning of a learning collocation from context.

## Learn

Core chunks:

- **set a goal** — decide what you want to achieve;
- **make progress** — improve over time;
- **pay attention** — focus carefully;
- **ask for feedback** — request comments that can help you improve;
- **improve a skill** — become better at an ability;
- **keep track of progress** — record or follow changes over time;
- **review notes** — look at notes again to remember or understand them;
- **solve a problem** — find an answer or way forward.

- set a goal = đặt mục tiêu;

- feedback = phản hồi;

# LESSON 4 — REA-B1-05

**Module:** reading  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Title:** Can short breaks help you learn?  
**Estimated time:** 25 minutes  
**Activities:** 8

## Objectives

1. identify the main claim of a balanced informational text;
2. distinguish supporting reasons from qualifications;
3. infer meaning from context;
4. use textual evidence to evaluate a recommendation.

## Passage

### CAN SHORT BREAKS HELP YOU LEARN?

When people study for a long time, they sometimes notice that they are still looking at the page but no longer thinking carefully about it. A short, planned break can help at this point. Standing up, stretching, getting some water or looking away from a screen for a few minutes may help a learner return with better attention.

However, this does not mean that every interruption is useful. A break can easily become much longer than planned. For example, checking one message on a phone may lead to ten minutes of videos or group-chat replies. In that case, the learner may find it harder to return to the original task.

One useful approach is to decide on a clear stopping point before beginning a study session. A learner might work until the end of a short section, then take a five-minute break. The exact timing does not need to be the same for everyone. A difficult maths problem may require a longer period of concentration, while reviewing vocabulary might work well in shorter blocks.

The important point is that breaks should support the task rather than replace it. If a learner notices attention beginning to drift, a brief planned pause may be useful. But if the learner is deeply focused and making progress, there may be no reason to stop simply because a timer says so.
