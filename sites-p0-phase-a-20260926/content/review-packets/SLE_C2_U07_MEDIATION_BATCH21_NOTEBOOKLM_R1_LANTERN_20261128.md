## 1. REA-C2-06 — Reconstruct changing positions and nested attribution

**CORE-1 — Goal/context.** Reconstruct a coherent account from a written discussion record, technical note and proposed minute. Identify exactly what speakers revise, retain or quote, distinguish a definitional conflict from a practical disagreement, and select a concise synthesis that preserves unresolved positions and authority.

**CORE-2 — Teach/model.**
- Keep a position ledger: source/speaker → proposition → scope → status (reported, endorsed, conceded, withdrawn, unresolved) → later revision. A speaker can report another person's conclusion without endorsing it.
- If a term appears to unite or divide speakers, paraphrase its meaning in each usage before declaring agreement or conflict. Test whether they still disagree after aligning definitions.
- A later concession may qualify a recommendation without withdrawing its purpose. In compressed indirect speech, attach each reporting verb to the right speaker and preserve the time and modality of the original claim. Avoid attributing an editor's synthesis to a source.
- Construct a thematic synthesis: common ground → different senses or evidential limits → surviving substantive issue → agreed action/authority. Do not collapse distinct positions into a consensus or reproduce all turns chronologically.
- Audit each sentence by asking who could reasonably recognise it as their position, and which clause carries the qualification.

Independent worked demonstration (not assessed): a fictional theatre discussion uses “complete” differently. The costume coordinator means all pieces are sewn; the stage manager means all have been fitted to performers. The director initially requests a full dress rehearsal but later accepts a rehearsal using substitute costumes, while retaining the fitting requirement before the public performance. A volunteer quotes the coordinator's “complete” in a newsletter as if the stage manager had confirmed readiness. Reconstruct: sewing is reported complete; fitting remains necessary; the director revised the rehearsal condition, not the public-performance requirement. The two uses of complete are not themselves a disagreement about whether fitting occurred. A precise synthesis attributes the sewing claim, distinguishes substitute rehearsal from performance readiness and does not imply that anyone authorised an unfitted costume for public use.

### Reading asset SRC-REA-C2-06 — A lantern archive's two meanings of recovery

[A] Technical note by conservation imaging lead Imani: The archive has scanned 40 painted lantern panels. A digital reconstruction makes some faded patterns distinguishable by combining several scans of the same panel. In this note, recovery means that a feature can be distinguished in the combined image; it does not mean that the physical pigment has been restored. Some faint outlines can be interpreted in more than one way. The technical team proposes attaching a confidence label to each interpreted feature. It has not assessed how an ordinary visitor would interpret the combined image without such labels.

[B] Extract from written meeting record, first discussion:
Curator Rowan: “I would not exhibit a recovered panel as though its original colours were intact. A visitor could take the combined image for a photograph of the object today. My initial preference is to show only photographs.”
Imani: “By recovered I mean a distinguishable feature in a digital image. I agree that a reconstruction must not be represented as the present physical state. Showing both the photograph and the labelled reconstruction would let visitors see a distinction that a photograph alone may not reveal.”
Visitor educator Sera: “One teacher told us that a reconstructed pattern might make the manufacturing technique easier to discuss. That is a suggestion from one teacher, not a test of what visitors learn. I would like a small guided session, where uncertain features can be explained.”

[C] Written meeting record, later discussion:
Rowan: “With the distinction between image recovery and physical restoration made explicit, I no longer object in principle to displaying the paired images in a guided session. I still oppose including reconstructed features in the unlabelled gallery display. Even a label may not be sufficient there if visitors never look at it.”
Imani: “I can support a guided session as a first step, but I would not rule out a later self-guided display if visitors can reliably distinguish the image from the object and notice the uncertainty labels.”
Sera: “My concern is that a short guided session may not reveal how people interpret the images on their own. I support starting there, provided we do not describe success in that setting as proof that unassisted viewing works.”
Secretary: The meeting agreed to propose one guided session to the archive board, with a short check of visitors' understanding of the image/object distinction and ambiguous features. The board has not approved the session. There is no agreement on whether a later self-guided display should be introduced.

[D] Proposed publicity minute, drafted by a communications volunteer: “The conservation team confirmed that the lanterns have been recovered. Teachers established the value of the restored colours for learning. The curator withdrew the objections to exhibition, and the team agreed that the successful guided session would lead to an unlabelled gallery display.” The minute has not been adopted.

## 2. REA-C2-07 — Mediate branching technical conditions without changing their effect

**CORE-1 — Goal/context.** Construct and audit expert and accessible accounts from a layered technical report, maintenance protocol and stakeholder note. Identify which simplifications change a permission, exception, uncertainty or practical implication; preserve distinct decision owners and the audience's required action.

**CORE-2 — Teach/model.**
- Make a shared invariant ledger: defined term, tested claim, condition/exception, action, owner, uncertainty and source. Mark details as essential for both readers, essential for one reader, or safely compressible.
- For a branching rule, write separate if/then lines and identify when an exception overrides the normal rule. Distinguish a necessary condition from a sufficient condition and distinguish permission from obligation.
- Expert mediation retains the technical definition and design limits that determine interpretation. Accessible mediation replaces jargon with a faithful explanation and supplies the practical action; it does not hide a restriction because it is inconvenient.
- In a short summary, preserve the force of conditional and contrastive language. Use attribution when a stakeholder's preference is not an operating rule.
- Audit the reformulation: original clause → new wording → preserved implication → omitted detail and reason. Check a counterexample at the boundary of the rule.

Independent worked demonstration (not assessed): a fictional fabric archive states that a scanner's “stable” indicator means the light output is within a tested range, not that a fragile cloth is safe to scan. Staff may scan ordinary samples when the light is stable and the handling check is complete; fragile samples require separate approval from the conservation lead even if both checks pass. Expert summary: preserve the tested light definition, both normal conditions and exception. Accessible notice: “Staff can scan ordinary cloth after two checks; fragile cloth must be separately cleared by the conservation lead.” If visitors merely request scans, say that a request is not clearance. Omitting the name of the light sensor may be harmless for visitors; omitting the fragile-cloth exception would allow an unauthorised action. Check the case of fragile cloth with both checks passed: the separate approval is still required.

### Reading asset SRC-REA-C2-07 — Water status at a community orchard

[A] Technical report: A new orchard notice system combines a moisture reading with a record of water delivery. The display status “within range” means the measured soil moisture at a particular sensor was within the trial's predefined limits at the time of measurement. It does not establish adequate moisture in every plot, root condition or safety of water for drinking. In a bench test, the system displayed the expected status for all 24 prepared sensor readings, including six out-of-range cases. The test did not examine sensor drift in the orchard or failures in transmitting recent readings. The team recommends a separate field comparison using manual readings in different plots. Water from the orchard tanks is designated for irrigation only, not for drinking.

[B] Current maintenance protocol, approved by the orchard manager:
Ordinary plots: a volunteer may carry out routine irrigation only if the plot's local display is within range, a timestamp is present and no more than two hours old, and the scheduled irrigation instruction is active. A green within-range display alone is not an instruction to water. If a timestamp is missing or older than two hours, or the display is out of range, the volunteer must pause and request a manual check from the duty supervisor. The supervisor may issue a specific irrigation instruction after that check.
Newly planted plots: regardless of display status, use only a specific instruction from the horticulture lead; the duty supervisor's routine-plot instruction does not substitute for that approval.
An announced field comparison is proposed, not approved. Until a change is approved, the current protocol applies.

[C] Volunteer coordinator's written note: “People have asked for a one-line explanation of the green display. My preference is to explain that the measured moisture was within the trial limits when checked, then direct volunteers to the instructions for their plot. Some volunteers would like a single green light that means water now. I understand the convenience, but we have not agreed to change the operating rule. The proposed field comparison may help us understand reliability; it would not by itself authorise changing the irrigation instructions.”

[D] Draft public notice: “The system has passed all safety checks. Green means your plot is safe and ready to water. When the comparison begins, supervisors can clear every plot for irrigation.” The orchard manager asks for a short volunteer notice and a separate technical briefing for staff planning the possible field comparison.

REA-C2-07-Q1 — Which technical statement preserves what the bench test established?
- A. It validated moisture distribution across every plot under routine field conditions. — Extends prepared readings to field distribution.
- B. It established that sensor drift cannot cause an erroneous display. — Sensor drift was not examined.
- C. It verified that water from the orchard tanks was safe to drink. — The test did not examine drinking safety; tanks are irrigation only.
- D. It verified the display's response to the 24 prepared readings, including six out-of-range cases, but did not establish field reliability or plot-wide moisture. — **Key D.** Preserves measure, scope and limits.

REA-C2-07-Q2 — Which volunteer-facing explanation of “within range” avoids changing its practical implication?
- A. The green display shows that the water is safe and watering is compulsory. — Confuses moisture, safety and obligation.
- B. The last local reading fell within the trial's moisture limits; check its age and the instruction for your plot before watering. — **Key B.** Explains the local, time-limited status and points to the governing rule.
- C. The whole plot has adequate moisture today, so no other instructions matter. — Overgeneralises the sensor reading and removes the protocol.
- D. Green certifies that the sensor cannot be wrong. — Invents field reliability.

REA-C2-07-Q3 — An ordinary plot has a green display and an active scheduled instruction, but no timestamp. Which instruction preserves [B]?
- A. Pause and request a manual check from the duty supervisor; resume only on a specific instruction after the check. — **Key A.** The missing timestamp triggers the override despite the other conditions.
- B. Proceed because green plus a schedule makes a timestamp optional. — Removes a required condition.
- C. Ask the horticulture lead to approve the field comparison before doing anything. — Confuses the role for newly planted plots and the proposed test.
- D. Water immediately, then request a timestamp from the technical team. — Reverses the required sequence.

REA-C2-07-Q4 — Which version correctly mediates the exception for newly planted plots?
- A. All plots may follow routine instructions as long as the display is green. — Erases the exception.
- B. The duty supervisor can replace the horticulture lead whenever a timestamp is missing. — The protocol expressly excludes that substitution.
- C. For newly planted plots, follow only a specific instruction from the horticulture lead, even if the display is green or a routine instruction exists. — **Key C.** Preserves the override and owner.
- D. Newly planted plots must not receive water under any circumstances. — Converts conditional permission into prohibition.

REA-C2-07-Q5 — The short volunteer notice cannot include every technical detail. Which omission is defensible for that notice while retaining the full briefing for staff?
- A. Omit the newly planted plot exception because most volunteers work on ordinary plots. — Could authorise the wrong action.
- B. Omit the irrigation-only restriction because a moisture indicator does not measure drinking safety. — The notice should not leave the draft's safety implication uncorrected.
- C. Omit the need for a current timestamp because volunteers can see the colour. — Removes a necessary condition.
- D. Omit the exact 24-case bench-test count, retaining the limited meaning of the display and the complete operational conditions and exceptions. — **Key D.** The sample detail matters to technical evaluation but is not needed for following the rule.

REA-C2-07-Q6 — Which two-audience plan best repairs [D] and leaves a transparent audit trail?
- A. Tell volunteers the system is reliable and explain all caveats only in the technical briefing. — Conceals material limits from those acting on the notice.
- B. Tell volunteers what green means, the ordinary-plot checks, the pause procedure, the separate newly planted plot instruction and irrigation-only restriction; tell staff additionally about test scope, field gaps and proposed comparison. Record that the sample count was omitted only from the short notice. — **Key B.** Preserves shared invariants, audience-specific details and an explicit omission reason.
- C. Present the coordinator's preferred single green light as approved and leave the protocol for staff only. — Confuses stakeholder preference, user request and authority.
- D. Give both audiences the draft unchanged because they can infer the restrictions themselves. — Leaves multiple material false implications.
