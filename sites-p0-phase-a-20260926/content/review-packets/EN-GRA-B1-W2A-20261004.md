## GRA-B1-02 — Present perfect simple or continuous?

**Module:** grammar  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 25 minutes

### Objectives

- Choose present perfect simple when a completed result or number is important.
- Choose present perfect continuous when duration or an ongoing/recent activity is important.
- Avoid continuous forms with common stative verbs such as know, own and believe.

### Explanation — English

Both forms connect the past with the present. Use the present perfect simple to focus on a result, a completed amount or a state: I've written five emails; We've owned this house since 2018. Use the present perfect continuous to focus on an activity, its duration, or visible recent activity: I've been studying for three hours; Your hands are dirty—have you been gardening? Some verbs, especially stative verbs such as know, own and believe, are normally not used in the continuous form.

### Vietnamese support

Cả hai thì đều nối quá khứ với hiện tại. Dùng present perfect simple khi nhấn mạnh kết quả, số lượng đã hoàn thành hoặc trạng thái. Dùng present perfect continuous khi nhấn mạnh hoạt động, thời lượng hoặc dấu hiệu của hoạt động vừa diễn ra. Các động từ trạng thái như know, own, believe thường không dùng ở dạng tiếp diễn.

### Examples

- RESULT · I have written five emails since lunch.
- DURATION · I have been writing emails for two hours.
- STATE · We have known each other for years.
- RECENT ACTIVITY · Your shoes are muddy. Have you been walking in the rain?

## GRA-B1-03 — Past perfect and narrative order

**Module:** grammar  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 25 minutes

### Objectives

- Use had + past participle for an action completed before another past time or action.
- Combine past perfect and past simple to make narrative order clear.
- Recognize common past participles used in past-perfect sentences.

### Explanation — English

Use the past perfect (had + past participle) when you need to look back from one past moment to an earlier past event. The later event is often in the past simple: When we arrived, the film had started. The form is especially useful when the order is not otherwise obvious, and with ideas such as already, never and by the time. Do not use the past perfect simply because two actions are in the past; use it when the earlier-before-later relationship matters.

### Vietnamese support

Dùng past perfect (had + past participle) khi từ một thời điểm trong quá khứ nhìn lùi về một sự việc đã xảy ra trước đó. Sự việc xảy ra sau thường dùng past simple. Thì này hữu ích với already, never, by the time và khi cần làm rõ thứ tự trước–sau.

### Examples

- When we arrived, the film had already started.
- I realized that I had left my wallet at home.
- By the time the bus came, we had waited for nearly an hour.
- She had never seen snow before she visited Korea.

## GRA-B1-04 — Past habits with used to and would

**Module:** grammar  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 24 minutes

### Objectives

- Use used to for past states or repeated actions that are no longer true.
- Use would for repeated past actions when the past-time context is clear.
- Form questions and negatives with did + use to.

### Explanation — English

Use used to + base verb for past states or repeated actions that are different now: I used to live near school; We used to play outside every evening. Would + base verb can describe repeated past actions when the past context is already clear: Every summer, my grandfather would take us fishing. In this meaning, would is normally not used for past states such as own, know or be. Questions and negatives use did: Did you use to walk to school? I didn't use to like coffee.

### Vietnamese support

Dùng used to + động từ nguyên mẫu cho trạng thái hoặc hành động lặp lại trong quá khứ nhưng nay đã khác. Would + động từ nguyên mẫu có thể nói về hành động lặp lại khi bối cảnh quá khứ đã rõ, nhưng thường không dùng would cho trạng thái như own, know, be. Câu hỏi/phủ định dùng did + use to.

### Examples

- STATE · I used to live near the river.
- HABIT · We used to play football after school.
- REPEATED ACTION · Every Sunday, Dad would make pancakes.
- QUESTION · Did you use to walk to school?

## GRA-B1-05 — Modal deduction: must, might and can't

**Module:** grammar  
**CEFR target:** B1  
**Estimated time:** 24 minutes

### Objectives

- Use must for a strong positive deduction based on present evidence.
- Use might, may or could for a possible but uncertain conclusion.
- Use can't for a strong negative deduction when evidence makes something impossible.

### Explanation — English

Modal verbs can express how sure we are about a present situation. Use must when the evidence makes a conclusion very likely: The lights are on and their car is outside; they must be home. Use might, may or could when something is possible but uncertain. Use can't when evidence makes a conclusion impossible: He is in Tokyo today, so he can't be in Hanoi. For negative deduction, don't use mustn't to mean 'I am sure it is not true'; use can't.

### Vietnamese support

Modal verbs có thể diễn tả mức độ chắc chắn khi suy luận về hiện tại. Dùng must khi bằng chứng khiến kết luận rất có khả năng đúng; might/may/could khi chỉ là khả năng; can't khi bằng chứng cho thấy điều đó không thể đúng. Không dùng mustn't với nghĩa suy luận 'chắc chắn không'; dùng can't.

### Examples

- STRONG POSITIVE · Their car is outside. They must be home.
- POSSIBLE · I don't know where Mia is. She might be in the library.
- STRONG NEGATIVE · That can't be Ben; Ben is much taller.
- POSSIBLE · This could be the correct bus, but let's check the number.
