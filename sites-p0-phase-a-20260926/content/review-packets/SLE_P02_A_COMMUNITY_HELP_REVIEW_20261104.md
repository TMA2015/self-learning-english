## P02-U01 — Find the right place to ask for help

**Module:** vocabulary · **Target:** Pre-A1 toward A1 · **Prerequisites:** recognize simple school/community places (library, school, park) and basic question forms; provide a simple picture/word review when needed.  
**CORE-1 goal/context:** Given a short everyday need, select a suitable community place or helper, then practise retrieving a polite phrase without a model. This does not assess spontaneous spoken fluency.

**CORE-2 teach:** A *library* is a place to borrow books; a *bus stop* is where you wait for a bus; an *information desk* is where you ask for directions or opening information; a *pharmacy* is where you ask a pharmacist about medicines (do not ask learners to share their medical information). Use the chunk **Where can I find ...?** to ask for a place and **Could you help me find ...?** to ask a person politely. `find` means locate, not purchase. A common mistake is to confuse the bus stop (waiting) with the information desk (asking).  
**Independent worked demonstration, NOT scored:** At a sports centre, a visitor wants to know which room has a swimming class. They ask at the reception desk: “Could you help me find the swimming pool?” The desk gives information; the visitor does not wait there for a bus. The example does not disclose any active scored scenario below.  
**Strategy:** Identify the need (borrow / wait / ask / find), choose the place that performs that function, and reread the whole question before answering.  
**CORE-3 practice:** Four independent tasks below. Objective recognition and phrase retrieval are different evidence types. Do not show keys until after submission.

## P02-U02 — Read a community notice and choose a safe next step

**Module:** reading · **Target:** A1 · **Prerequisite:** P02-U01 or equivalent vocabulary; can read a short time/place notice.  
**CORE-1 goal/context:** Understand the purpose, time and change in a short public notice, then identify a reasonable next action. This is written Reading, **not Listening**.

**CORE-2 teach:** Read once for the main purpose (what is happening), then look for the *latest* time and place. A change marker such as **instead** replaces an earlier instruction. A notice may state a fact directly; a sensible next action can be inferred, but an inference must be supported by the notice.  
**Independent worked demonstration, NOT scored:** “The chess club usually meets in Room 3. On Tuesday, meet in Room 7 instead. Bring a pencil.” The main idea is a room change. Room 7 is current; a learner should bring a pencil. This demonstration is unrelated to the graded notice below.  
**Strategy:** (1) Summarize the notice in five words. (2) Underline the latest day/time/place. (3) Distinguish what the text *says* from what it reasonably *suggests*.

**CORE-3 independent reading passage (original, student-visible):**

> COMMUNITY GARDEN DAY  
> Families can help plant flowers on Saturday. We will start at 10:00 a.m. near the small gate of Green Park. Please bring a water bottle. You do not need to bring garden tools; the organisers will provide them. If it rains, the event will move to the indoor community hall next to the park. Check the notice board on Saturday morning for the location.
