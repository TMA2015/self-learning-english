## 1. REA-B2-15 — Compare sources before drawing a conclusion

**CORE-1: Learning goal and situation.** You are advising a volunteer exhibition committee. Compare a report about observed visitor behaviour with feedback from a different group. Identify what each source actually supports, distinguish a reported result from an explanation, and select a limited next step. B2; suggested 30–35 minutes.

**CORE-2: Teach and model (different context).** Skim each source for its purpose; then build a mental map: *who / what was observed / what was not measured / what the writer recommends*. Compare the measures before comparing numbers. If two sources cover different groups, neither must be discarded, but their numbers cannot automatically be added or treated as a representative sample. **Independent mini-model:** A school garden report counts 70 visits during lunchtime. A separate caretaker note describes water use and says several plots need extra attention. A careful summary says the garden attracted visits but workload needs investigation; it does not say 70 different students visited, or that popularity caused the maintenance problem. **Independent self-check:** Is “the garden needs maintenance because 70 people visited” justified? Compare your answer with this teaching example: no; a causal link is not established. This unscored example is unrelated to the assessed sources.

**CORE-3: Assessed sources (original fictional texts).**

### Source A — Report from the Alder Quay photograph exhibition

The Alder Quay exhibition team tested two ways of guiding visitors around its temporary display of local photographs. During the first weekend, a printed route sheet was available at the entrance. On the second weekend, volunteers also offered a short introduction at fixed times. The route sheet remained available throughout both weekends.

Staff recorded 180 entries over the first weekend and 212 over the second. These figures count entries, not individual visitors; someone who came back twice could be counted twice. Staff also asked a small convenience sample of people leaving the hall which part of the exhibition they had spent most time in. During the second weekend, more of the people interviewed mentioned the upstairs display. The team found this encouraging, because that display had previously drawn fewer comments. However, it did not record how long visitors actually spent upstairs, and the interviewers did not approach people who left by the side exit.

The report recommends testing the introduction on a few additional days before deciding whether to make it a regular feature. It explicitly notes that a community market took place nearby on the second weekend, and that the team cannot separate the effect of the introduction from other differences between weekends.

### Source B — Volunteer coordinator's note on access

The introduction was useful for visitors who wanted an overview, but a fixed-time activity was not convenient for everybody. Three volunteers reported helping people who arrived just after an introduction and could not wait for the next one. One family suggested a large-print version of the route sheet. Another visitor asked whether the welcome could be offered without requiring people to climb stairs first. These are examples of difficulties the coordinator heard about, not a survey of all visitors.

The coordinator suggests keeping the printed sheet, making a large-print version and testing a short welcome which can be given at the entrance when a volunteer is free. She does not recommend removing the upstairs display. The second-weekend staff rota included an extra helper borrowed from a different event, so the regular team has not yet established whether it can provide introductions every weekend.

**REA-B2-15-Q1** (reading.gist). Which statement best represents the two sources together?
A. Both reports show that the upstairs exhibition should be removed.
B. Both support investigating ways to guide visitors, while identifying limits to the evidence and practical delivery.
C. Both prove that the second weekend attracted more individual visitors because of the introduction.
D. Both recommend replacing printed information with fixed-time introductions.
**Key:** B. **Rationale:** Source A supports further testing and acknowledges confounding differences; B describes specific access and staffing issues and suggests a flexible welcome alongside paper. **Misconception:** treating a shared interest in guidance as agreement on one permanent method. **Repair:** distinguish common aim from proposed methods.

**REA-B2-15-Q2** (reading.detail). What exactly did the team count across the two weekends?
A. Entries to the exhibition hall, including possible repeat entries
B. Unique visitors who joined a volunteer introduction
C. Visitors who went to the upstairs display for ten minutes or more
D. People who responded to the same survey question on both weekends
**Key:** A. **Rationale:** The report explicitly defines the count as entries, not individuals. **Misconception:** treating a count of visits as a count of people. **Repair:** underline the measured unit, not the larger number.

**REA-B2-15-Q3** (reading.inference). Why does the nearby community market matter to the team's interpretation?
A. It makes the visitor comments unusable as qualitative feedback.
B. It shows that the introduction was not useful to anyone.
C. It proves the first weekend's route sheets were incomplete.
D. It provides another plausible explanation for differences in entries between weekends.
**Key:** D. **Rationale:** The market is a difference that may have affected attendance; the report says the effects cannot be separated. **Misconception:** assuming a change occurring after an introduction was caused by it. **Repair:** name the observed change and one competing explanation separately.

**REA-B2-15-Q4** (reading.detail). Which feature of the exit interviews limits what can be concluded about time spent upstairs?
A. The questions were asked at the beginning of each visit.
B. The printed sheet was unavailable during the second weekend.
C. Staff obtained mentions from a small group leaving through the main route rather than timing visits or approaching everyone.
D. The team counted only people already working as exhibition volunteers.
**Key:** C. **Rationale:** The report describes convenience sampling, excluded side-exit visitors, and no time measurement. **Misconception:** treating what interviewees mention as measured time for all visitors. **Repair:** separate response content, who was asked and what was not recorded.

**REA-B2-15-Q5** (reading.inference). Which interpretation of Source B is best supported?
A. The coordinator rejects all volunteer introductions because some people arrived late.
B. The coordinator wants to reduce barriers to receiving guidance without assuming the regular team can maintain the current arrangement.
C. The coordinator thinks large-print sheets will solve every access difficulty.
D. The coordinator is asking the team to close the upstairs display until next year.
**Key:** B. **Rationale:** She proposes a flexible entrance welcome and large-print sheets and raises unresolved staffing capacity; she does not reject introductions or the upstairs display. **Misconception:** reading a practical concern as opposition to the aim itself. **Repair:** pair a suggested change with its stated reason and constraint.

**REA-B2-15-Q6** (reading.inference). Which next step makes best use of both sources without overstating the findings?
A. Trial the printed and large-print routes plus flexible welcomes on additional days; record entry counts, collect broader access feedback and monitor staffing before deciding.
B. Make fixed introductions compulsory immediately because entries rose by 32.
C. Ask only satisfied visitors to complete a survey and use their answers to represent all visitors.
D. Remove all printed sheets so the team can compare the two original weekends more accurately.
**Key:** A. **Rationale:** It responds to both sources' recommendations and limitations without promising a causal conclusion or permanent change. **Misconception:** jumping from tentative evidence to an irreversible plan. **Repair:** match one action to each source constraint and retain a review point.

**CORE-4: Post-submission feedback and next step.** Review the exact authored rationale per answered item only after submission. Label the mistake as measurement, inference, sampling, position, or proportionate action; redo with a separate context: a sports club counts equipment loans one week and asks a few volunteers about waiting times the following week. Which findings can be stated together without asserting that equipment availability caused every wait? Write one unscored sentence. Suggested self-check: retain the counts and reports as different kinds of evidence, and state what remains unknown. A retry is practice, not a second independent CEFR proof. Optional exam-transfer strategy (no exam score): identify the source and scope before choosing the best-supported paraphrase; avoid treating an absolute statement as correct just because it contains familiar words.

## 2. LIS-B2-01 — Listen for a qualified decision and speaker positioning

**CORE-1: Learning goal and listening situation.** Follow a short discussion between an organizer and a venue/volunteer coordinator about taking an exhibition to other venues. Identify purpose, practical conditions, speaker stance and the final decision. B2; suggested 25–30 minutes. **Author/reviewer only: the assessed script below is NOT learner-facing before graded submit.**

**CORE-2: Teach the listening process.** On a first listen, identify the decision the group needs to make. On another focused listen, listen for shifts in position, reasons, contrast and conditions (*I would support that if…; that is not the same as…; before we commit…*). These phrases signal relationships, not fixed answers to look for. Take short notes if useful. **Separate written language example (not listening evidence):** In a meeting about a sports club's noticeboard, a volunteer says, “I like the idea, but only if we can display it indoors.” The speaker supports the idea conditionally rather than rejecting it. Do not use this example to guess the assessed dialogue. No mandatory number of replays, no transcript before graded submission, and no speed challenge as a proxy for proficiency.

**CORE-3: Original assessed script — reviewer and production use only.**

> **Host / organizer (Mara):** The Fern Bridge archive has been asked to take a small display to three neighbourhood halls. We have enough transport funding for two visits this term. Before choosing venues, we need to decide what a useful first trial would look like, not promise every hall a visit.
>
> **Venue and volunteer coordinator (Jon):** The North Hall offered us Saturday mornings and expects a fairly large audience because its craft group meets then. The East Hall suggested a Tuesday afternoon. It is much smaller, but the manager says some people there rarely travel to the main archive. If the point is to reach people who do not already visit us, a large crowd is not the only thing to consider.
>
> **Mara:** Can the volunteers manage that combination with our existing display?
>
> **Jon:** I can provide a team on one Saturday and one Tuesday, but the boxes need to be lighter. Our previous display used glass cases, and moving those safely would take more people than we have. I'd support both halls if we use printed copies on lightweight panels rather than original objects.
>
> **Mara:** That makes sense. We also need a sensible way of evaluating the visits. The managers could tell us attendance, but those numbers alone wouldn't reveal whether we reached new visitors. Could we ask one optional, anonymous question about whether someone has visited the archive before?
>
> **Jon:** Yes, provided we make it clear that people can enjoy the display without answering. We should not compare raw attendance and call the larger event more successful. The venues have different opening hours and capacities.
>
> **Mara:** Let's also confirm that both venues can accommodate the lightweight panels before we book transport.
>
> **Jon:** Yes. That gives us a realistic plan to take back to the venue managers.
>
> **Mara:** Agreed. We will prepare a two-venue trial plan for North and East, check panel safety and venue agreement, then book only if those checks pass. We'll review attendance, voluntary first-visit feedback and the team's workload afterwards. We haven't made a permanent tour commitment.

**Proposed media production:** original English two-speaker dialogue, with distinct voices for Mara and Jon; retain the words and turn order exactly. Natural, intelligible delivery at B2-appropriate pace with brief turn pauses. No added narration, feedback, answers, filenames spoken aloud or music. If two speakers cannot be produced faithfully, do not silently rewrite roles or combine turns; obtain a reviewed script revision or use an authorised recording. Measure actual duration and SHA-256 only after the real MP3 exists; no guessed manifest values. Maintain speaker labels in internal production transcript, not pre-submit learner display. Verify audio licensing and ownership per standard.

**LIS-B2-01-Q1** (listening.gist). What is the main purpose of the discussion?
A. To design a permanent archive tour covering every hall
B. To compare the archive's ticket prices with other venues
C. To plan a limited trial and establish conditions for proceeding and evaluating it
D. To decide which archive objects should be sold
**Key:** C. **Rationale:** Mara sets the planning purpose and limited funding; the final decision is a conditional two-venue trial. **Misconception:** interpreting a request from three halls as an accepted commitment. **Repair:** on post-submit replay, listen to the opening purpose and final statement together.

**LIS-B2-01-Q2** (listening.detail). What does Jon identify as a reason to consider the East Hall despite its smaller size?
A. Its visitors may include people who seldom travel to the main archive.
B. It has promised a larger craft-group audience than North Hall.
C. It provides free transport for the original glass cases.
D. It opens every morning and evening throughout the trial.
**Key:** A. **Rationale:** Jon says some East Hall people rarely travel to the main archive; reaching them could matter more than crowd size. **Misconception:** treating capacity as the sole selection criterion. **Repair:** listen for the contrast after “much smaller” and the intended audience.

**LIS-B2-01-Q3** (listening.speaker_intention). What is Jon's position on the practical feasibility of visiting both halls?
A. She prefers to display original objects regardless of staffing.
B. She refuses both visits because volunteers are unavailable.
C. She will support both only if the visits take place on Saturdays.
D. She supports both conditionally, as long as lighter printed displays replace the difficult-to-move original objects.
**Key:** D. **Rationale:** She can staff one Saturday and one Tuesday and explicitly conditions support on lightweight panels with printed copies. **Misconception:** hearing a logistical objection as complete rejection. **Repair:** replay the sentence starting “I'd support both halls if…”, after submission.

**LIS-B2-01-Q4** (listening.detail). What information would Mara's proposed optional question collect?
A. Whether visitors want to become paid archive members
B. Whether visitors have visited the archive before
C. Whether visitors would rather use glass cases or panels
D. How many minutes visitors can stay at each venue
**Key:** B. **Rationale:** Mara proposes a voluntary, anonymous first-visit question to complement attendance. **Misconception:** confusing evaluation of reach with evaluation of every aspect of the experience. **Repair:** contrast the question's actual focus with the broader trial goals.

**LIS-B2-01-Q5** (listening.inference). Suppose the North trial later records 80 attendees and the East trial records 35, but the voluntary feedback has not yet been examined. Which conclusion would be consistent with the speakers' evaluation plan?
A. North was more effective at reaching people unfamiliar with the archive.
B. East was necessarily more successful because its venue was smaller.
C. North recorded higher attendance, but relative success in reaching new visitors is not yet established.
D. Both events reached the same number of new visitors because both used printed copies.
**Key:** C. **Rationale:** The hypothetical figures show different attendance totals; Jon cautions against treating raw counts as success, and Mara proposes separate information about prior visits. None of the other options follows from the stated plan. **Misconception:** interpreting a larger total as necessarily greater success. **Repair:** distinguish the observed count from the intended audience outcome and identify what more information the team needs.

**LIS-B2-01-Q6** (listening.detail). What is authorized by the final decision?
A. Booking all three halls immediately, then checking safety later
B. Preparing a plan for North and East and booking only after panel safety and venue agreement are confirmed
C. Replacing the main archive with a permanent travelling display
D. Booking the North Hall alone and dropping the evaluation
**Key:** B. **Rationale:** Mara's final statement records preparation, checks and conditional booking, not a permanent tour. **Misconception:** treating preparation as unconditional booking. **Repair:** replay the final sentence and list the action and its prerequisites separately.

## 3. WRI-B2-12 — Turn mixed evidence into a qualified recommendation

**CORE-1: Learning goal.** Draft and revise a clear recommendation for a community archive committee, based on two fresh written sources. Preserve evidence limits, answer a fair objection and propose an actionable review. B2; 35–45 minutes. This is a separate task and does not use or reproduce the preceding Reading answers or Listening transcript.

**CORE-2: Teach the writing process.**
- **Topic:** identify the actual issue, not merely the broad theme. **Task:** identify genre, reader and requested decision. **Must Include:** extract required points from the prompt. **Boundary:** select claims supported by the sources; do not invent results, people or permanent decisions.
- Brainstorm possible positions, select only relevant points, outline *recommendation → evidence from both sources → limitation/objection → response → next step*. Choose one meaningful objection, not a weak claim that nobody makes. Distinguish modal certainty and reporting verbs (e.g., *suggests, reported, may, provided that*). Revise a sentence that makes a stronger claim than its evidence permits.
- **Independent annotated model (different prompt):** A youth orchestra must decide whether to trial a rehearsal reminder. Separate model sources: nine players told a conductor they missed a changed rehearsal time; a secretary says reminder messages add work and some players do not use the messaging app. Example: “I recommend a two-week trial of printed and electronic reminders [limited decision + concrete action]. The conductor's reports suggest that changed times are sometimes missed, although nine reports do not establish how often this happens across the orchestra [attribution + limitation]. The secretary's concern about workload is reasonable; a shared weekly template and a printed copy would keep both routes available [fair objection + response]. We should review missed rehearsals and preparation time before adopting the system permanently [two relevant measures + review].” This is a worked miniature, not a length template or a model answer to the assessed task below. An alternative recommendation could be defensible with reasons.

**CORE-3: Original assessed source pair (fictional).**

**Source A — Millbrook Community Archive workshop note.** A Saturday workshop tested a small hands-on display in which visitors could handle reproduction objects and contribute questions to a public noticeboard. The team offered 40 places and recorded 32 attendees. Of the 18 attendees who voluntarily completed a feedback slip, 14 said they would like another hands-on activity. A teacher who observed the session said the handling activities prompted some thoughtful questions from younger visitors. The team did not count which attendees had visited the archive before, and it did not ask people who could not attend why they stayed away. There has been no weekday test.

**Source B — Access and staffing memo.** The archive has funds for one four-week pilot, not a permanent programme. It could run a hands-on session on Saturdays using two trained volunteers, or run shorter weekday drop-in displays using one volunteer and printed question cards. The existing rota can support two Saturdays in the four-week period or four weekday drop-ins; it cannot support both formats together with current staff. The weekday format has not yet been tested with participants. Two parents have asked whether younger children could attend without handling fragile objects. Any programme must provide clear supervision rules and a non-handling way to take part. The coordinator wants to review staff time, participation and access feedback before recommending a longer programme.

**WRI-B2-12-Q1 (extended response; UNSCORED).** Write a **180–240-word** recommendation to the Millbrook Community Archive committee. Recommend one available four-week pilot (two Saturday hands-on sessions or four weekday drop-in displays), not a combined programme. Explain your choice using accurate details from **both** sources; identify at least one limitation in the workshop feedback; address a fair objection or trade-off to your choice; include the supervision and non-handling safeguards; and propose at least two relevant measures plus a decision point at the end of the pilot. Use a formal but accessible register and coherent paragraphs. Do not claim the weekday format is proven effective or that all visitors prefer hands-on activities. Word range is planning guidance only.

**Four-part task model for learner:** Topic — access and delivery of an archive activity. Task — recommendation to the committee. Must Include — one feasible format, two-source evidence, limitation, fair response, safeguards, two review measures and next decision. Boundary — four-week funding and staffing limit; no invented effectiveness or universal preference; 180–240 words as guidance. Leave the actual position, source selection, reasoning and prose to the learner.

**Assessment contract:** Existing `RUBRIC-WRITING-B2` by reference only. `answer:null`; `evidence_weight:0`; trusted qualitative review required; before valid review show `UNASSESSED — trusted review pending`. No numeric band, score or CEFR outcome from word count, connectors, self-checks, planning slots, or an unchecked AI response. Do not claim provider availability. Optional AI follows existing opt-in validated writing-specific provider and saved-draft rules only. A real response can choose either available option and make a qualified case; no single model recommendation is required.

**CORE-4: Revise and transfer.** First check the task boundary, accurate report of the voluntary feedback denominator (18), feasibility, safeguards and review measures. Next choose one paragraph and improve the claim, reasoning or register. Optional unscored change log: “Original wording / revision / reason”. The learner can compare her revisions without overwriting the submitted original.
