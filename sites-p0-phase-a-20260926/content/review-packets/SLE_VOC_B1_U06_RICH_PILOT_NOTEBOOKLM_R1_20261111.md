## 1. Why this pilot is needed

The actual existing `VOC-B1-01` is 14 minutes with two example sentences and five objective questions. It introduces `reduce waste`, `reusable bottle`, `save energy`, `public transport` and `make a difference`. The target pilot **does not replace, rewrite, or regrade** that original lesson. It broadens the learner's useful repertoire with context, meaning, natural combinations, active recall and differentiated transfer.

The pilot must stand on its own even when Gemini is offline or legally unavailable. It must not assert that using a word once establishes B1 mastery. It must not turn a poster, illustration or quiz score into a formal assessment.

## 2. CORE-1 — Goal and meaningful context

**Lesson title:** Small environmental choices: useful phrases, not isolated words

**Learner-facing goal:** By the end, you should understand and use several natural English phrases to describe everyday environmental choices, suggest realistic actions and explain their possible effects without claiming that a small change proves a large result.

**Scenario:** Your school is planning a community fair. The committee wants to reduce unwanted materials and help visitors make practical choices. You need to explain what people can do, why it might help, and what information the committee still needs.

**What counts as progress:** You can select a natural phrase in a new context, remember some useful phrases without seeing a multiple-choice list, and write a short suggestion in your own words. This is practice evidence, not a CEFR certificate.

## 3. CORE-2 — Teach the vocabulary, notice patterns and practise safely

### A. Themed word and phrase bank (14 target chunks)

| # | Phrase | Learner-friendly meaning | Fresh model sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | reduce waste | produce less material that is thrown away | The cafeteria wants to reduce waste by offering portions in different sizes. |
| 2 | reusable container | a box or bottle that can be used many times | Hana brings her salad in a reusable container instead of a disposable box. |
| 3 | single-use packaging | wrapping or containers designed to be used once | The biscuits arrive in single-use packaging, so the club is checking alternatives. |
| 4 | sort waste | separate unwanted material by type | Visitors sort waste into labelled paper and food bins. |
| 5 | repair rather than replace | fix something instead of buying a new one | The library chose to repair its older chairs rather than replace them. |
| 6 | save energy | use less electricity or fuel than necessary | Switching off empty-room lights can save energy. |
| 7 | public transport | buses, trains and similar shared travel services open to the public | The volunteers used public transport to reach the community event. |
| 8 | cut down on | reduce the amount or frequency of something | We could cut down on printing by sharing one timetable per team. |
| 9 | have an impact on | affect a person, place or situation | The new footpath could have an impact on nearby plants. |
| 10 | raise awareness of | help more people learn about an issue | Students designed a notice to raise awareness of water waste. |
| 11 | make a difference | have a noticeable or useful effect | Clearer signs might make a difference, but we need to check. |
| 12 | take practical steps | do specific, realistic things rather than only discuss an issue | The committee took practical steps such as adding a refill station. |
| 13 | conserve water | use water carefully to avoid wasting it | Rainwater collected from a roof can help conserve water during dry weeks. |
| 14 | long-term effect | a result that continues or appears after a longer time | The class wants to measure the long-term effect of using refill taps. |

**Notice the chunks:** `reduce + waste`; `save + energy`; `raise + awareness of`; `have an impact on`; `cut down on`; `repair rather than replace`. A phrase is not a bag of interchangeable single words.

**Form and register:** `reusable` combines `re-` (again) and `usable`; `single-use` is commonly used before nouns such as `packaging`. `Cut down on` is natural in everyday discussion; `reduce` can sound more concise in a formal report. `Have an impact on` does not say whether the impact is positive or negative.

**Meaning caution:** `might make a difference` expresses possibility. A poster, a one-day activity or a few positive comments do not automatically prove a lasting environmental effect. Ask: What changed? Who was observed? For how long?

### B. Independent worked model — different setting from scored tasks

A youth sports centre is deciding whether to lend sports equipment rather than ask each visitor to buy new equipment. A careful suggestion is:

> "The centre could reuse equipment and repair damaged items where it is safe to do so. These steps might reduce waste. We should record how much equipment is used again before claiming a long-term effect."

**Why it works:** It names concrete actions, chooses natural collocations, uses `might` instead of a guaranteed causal claim, and asks for a relevant observation. This centre/equipment example is not assessed in the items below.

### C. How to study this bank in a single session

1. **Understand:** Read a phrase, its meaning and the model sentence; identify whether it describes an action, an object or an effect.
2. **Notice:** Underline the exact word partnership, such as `raise awareness of`.
3. **Retrieve:** Cover the English phrase column; use the meaning and a new situation to say the phrase aloud or write it from memory.
4. **Transfer:** Use the phrase in a **different** school/community situation, not by copying the model sentence.
5. **Revisit:** At the start of a later session, try to recall five phrases without viewing the bank. Review the ones you could not retrieve.

**Common pitfalls:** `make a difference` (not *do a difference*); `raise awareness` (not *rise awareness*); `cut down on waste` (not *cut down waste*); `an impact on` (not *an impact to*).

### Objective MCQ items

**VOC-B1-07-Q01.** At a drama festival, visitors bring food boxes that are washed and used at every rehearsal. What best describes these boxes?

- A. single-use packaging
- B. reusable containers
- C. long-term effects
- D. public transport

**VOC-B1-07-Q02.** A community art room fixes a broken table and continues using it instead of buying another. Which phrase describes the decision?

- A. repair rather than replace
- B. raise awareness of
- C. sort waste
- D. cut down on travel

**VOC-B1-07-Q03.** A museum asks visitors to put paper in one bin and food scraps in another. What are visitors being asked to do?

- A. conserve energy
- B. save transport
- C. sort waste
- D. make packaging

**VOC-B1-07-Q04.** The hiking group wants to use fewer printed maps while keeping one paper copy for safety. What natural phrase best completes the plan: "We could ___ printing"?

- A. take more on
- B. have an impact to
- C. make a difference to
- D. cut down on

**VOC-B1-07-Q05.** An exhibition explains why some rivers become polluted. The posters do not measure whether behaviour changes. What can the exhibition reasonably aim to do?

- A. prove that all pollution has stopped
- B. raise awareness of river pollution
- C. confirm the long-term effect of each poster
- D. replace every public transport service

**VOC-B1-07-Q06.** Which statement uses environmental vocabulary **without claiming more than the evidence shows**?

- A. One recycling poster proves everyone reduced waste permanently.
- B. Any change to a bin automatically conserves all local water.
- C. Clearer labels might make a difference, but the organisers should measure what visitors actually put in the bins.
- D. A single positive comment shows a guaranteed long-term effect.

**VOC-B1-07-Q07.** A town is considering a new walkway near a wildlife area. The committee is unsure whether it will help or harm local plants. Which phrase fits the uncertainty?

- A. have an impact on local plants
- B. become a single-use local plant
- C. sort the wildlife into buses
- D. repair the long-term impact rather than replace

**VOC-B1-07-Q08.** At a science fair, visitors can travel on city buses or a local train, which are open to everyone. What is this travel category?

- A. a reusable container
- B. a private home delivery
- C. an electrical appliance
- D. public transport

**VOC-B1-07-Q09.** A school has used less paper for one week, but nobody has checked what happens over the next year. Which statement is best supported?

- A. The one-week observation proves the long-term effect.
- B. The long-term effect remains unknown; the school needs information over a longer period.
- C. The word `effect` can only describe a bad result.
- D. Because the trial lasted one week, it had no possible impact at all.

**VOC-B1-07-Q10.** Students agree that their meeting creates too much rubbish. Which response best illustrates **taking practical steps**?

- A. Announce that the school has solved the problem forever.
- B. Say the same slogan each week without changing anything.
- C. Set up a water-refill point and count disposable cups before and after the change.
- D. Avoid discussing any possible action until the next school year.

### Short-answer retrieval (scored only by deterministic accepted answer)

**VOC-B1-07-Q11.** Complete with **one verb**: "The club hopes to ___ awareness of safe battery disposal."

**VOC-B1-07-Q12.** Complete with **one word**: "To reduce rubbish, we should cut ___ on single-use cups."

### Unscored personal transfer (no CEFR grade; not one of the 12 scored questions)

Choose **three or four** phrases from the Word Bank. Write 3–4 original sentences recommending *realistic* changes for an after-school club that wants to use fewer disposable materials. Include one sentence explaining what information the club should collect before claiming success. This is a self-practice prompt, **not** an exact-match scored item; neither word count nor model generation establishes writing ability.

## 5. CORE-4 — Reviewer-only answer key, explanations and next actions

**Never display this section before the learner submits.**

| ID | Key / accepted response | Explanation after submission | Next action if incorrect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q01 | B | The boxes can be washed and used repeatedly. | Contrast `reusable` with `single-use` by naming the number of uses. |
| Q02 | A | Repairing a usable table avoids replacing it with a new one. | Retell the decision using `repair rather than replace`. |
| Q03 | C | Separating unwanted material into categories means sorting waste. | Name two categories and decide how to sort them. |
| Q04 | D | `Cut down on` naturally means reduce the amount of an activity. | Practise `cut down on + noun/-ing` in a different sentence. |
| Q05 | B | The posters can inform visitors; the prompt provides no behaviour measurements. | Separate `raise awareness` from `prove behaviour changed`. |
| Q06 | C | The cautious modal and proposed measurement respect the evidence limit. | Rewrite a strong claim using `might` and a measurable follow-up. |
| Q07 | A | A walkway may affect plants positively or negatively; impact is neutral. | Use `have an impact on` with a different person/place. |
| Q08 | D | Buses and trains open to the public are examples of public transport. | Contrast shared public services with private travel. |
| Q09 | B | A short result cannot establish effects over a longer period. | Explain what extra time period you would measure. |
| Q10 | C | Installing a refill point and comparing counts are specific feasible actions. | Name one action and one measurement, not just a slogan. |
| Q11 | raise | The collocation is `raise awareness of`. | Say and write the whole chunk without looking at the Word Bank. |
| Q12 | down | The phrasal verb is `cut down on`. | Produce a new example with `cut down on + -ing`. |
