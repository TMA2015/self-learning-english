## 2. A1-U10: A friendly invitation based on two new notices

**Proposed learner outcome:** Find purpose, time, place, condition and necessary materials in simple written/spoken notices; choose an appropriate activity; write a short friendly message. Approximately 25–35 minutes across optional sessions. Scenario, person and venues are fictional.

### 2.1 CORE-1 — Student-facing start

> **Goal.** Two activities are happening on Saturday. Read one notice and listen to a short announcement. Find the details a new student needs, then write an invitation. Reading/listening choices can be checked; your own writing will be saved for feedback, not automatically given a language level.

Useful prior learning: simple times and days, invitations, *do not need to*, reading short notices, listening for location/time.

### 2.2 CORE-2 — Independent model (not used by the questions)

**Different written example:** “The chess club meets on Monday in Room 4 at 3:15. Bring a notebook. Students can borrow chess sets.”

- First ask **What is the message for?** It tells students how to join a club.
- For **where**, read the room phrase: *Room 4*, not the time.
- For **what to bring**, distinguish *a notebook* (asked for) from *chess sets* (available to borrow). The words “borrow” do not mean “buy.”

**Different listening example (TEACHING MODEL ONLY):** Instructor says, “The painting group starts at two in Art Room 1. Please bring an old shirt.” The key details are *two* and *Art Room 1*, not a number guessed from a previous text. If audio is missing, Listening cannot be scored. The model must be delivered through verified media if presented as an actual audio demonstration.

**Different writing example:** Task: send a short invitation to a classmate for an art exhibition. Topic: the exhibition; Task: invite; Must Include: day, place, one reason; Boundary: polite, 30–50 words, fictional context. Plan: greeting → invitation → details → question. Model sentence on the **art exhibition**, not on the Saturday activities below: “Hi Jo! There is an art exhibition at our school on Tuesday. It starts at four in the main hall. ## 3. A2-U10: Choose a practical community activity

**Proposed learner outcome:** Compare a short community notice with a caller's needs, conditions and limits; choose a suitable action and send a realistic short reply. Approximately 30–40 minutes across optional sessions.

### 3.1 CORE-1 — Student-facing goal

> **Goal.** Your friend needs practical help this weekend. Read an events notice and listen to their message. Find an option that fits the time and problem; notice what the event does **not** promise. Write a helpful reply. Your choices are checked only with evidence; your original message is not automatically scored for writing proficiency.

Useful prior learning: time expressions, schedules, *if / may / might*, everyday services, message planning.

### 3.2 CORE-2 — Independent model on a different topic

**Mini notice (model only):** “The book exchange opens on Wednesday at 4 p.m. in West Hall. Borrowing is free, but please bring your student card. Some books may not be available every week.”

**Separate model caller:** “I can go on Wednesday at five. I want to borrow a mystery novel, but I am not sure they will have one.”

Think aloud: Wednesday five falls **within** opening time only if the closing time permits; the notice does not supply a closing time, so **we cannot guarantee that five is possible**. “Some books may not be available” means no promise about the mystery novel. Do not turn a possibility into a certainty. For a message, analyse Topic/Task/Must Include/Boundary, note verified facts first, then include a realistic limitation rather than an invented promise. This model must **not** be added to the scored weekend question bank.

## 4. B1-U10: Recommend a responsible school pilot

**Proposed learner outcome:** Understand competing arguments and evidence limits, accurately summarise viewpoints from a written briefing and a spoken student perspective, and write a justified recommendation acknowledging a constraint. Approximately 35–45 minutes in one or two optional sessions.

### 4.1 CORE-1 — Student-facing goal

> **Goal.** Your school can try **one** eight-week project. Read a briefing about two possible rooms and listen to a student council comment. Separate facts, opinions and limits. Recommend a sensible pilot with evidence from both sources; your own argument is saved for review, not automatically certified as B1 Writing.

Useful prior learning: comparison, opinion vs evidence, conditionals, *however / although*, source limits, paragraph structure and revision.

### 4.2 CORE-2 — Independent reasoning model on a different issue

**Model written evidence:** “A survey of 60 students found that 38 would use a new drinking-water station. Installation is possible near the sports hall, but maintenance requires a weekly check.”  
**Separate model comment:** “I support the station, but we should check maintenance costs before deciding.”

Think aloud: The survey provides a **reason** for interest, not proof that all 60 students agree or that the project can operate without expense. The comment supports the idea **conditionally**. A reasoned recommendation states the evidence, a limitation and a feasible review/check. A claim such as “everyone wants the station” is unsound. This model is **not** the library/studio scenario assessed below.

**Different writing-model structure (not a sample answer to the active checkpoint):** Position → evidence from source 1 → source 2's caution → feasible condition/review → balanced closing. Brief model phrasing: “I recommend a trial of the water station because more than half the surveyed students showed interest. However, this does not prove demand across the entire school. Before a full rollout, the team should confirm maintenance and review use during the trial.”
