We have finished the first month of a small book-exchange trial at the community library. The exchange is designed for people who want to pass on a book they have read and choose another one, without needing to buy anything. We wanted to find out whether the arrangement was easy to use, not whether it increased the amount of reading in the whole neighbourhood.

We collected two kinds of information. First, the volunteers kept a record of books placed on the exchange shelf. They recorded forty-six additions over four Saturdays. That number tells us how often books were added. It does not tell us how many different people contributed, because the same person could bring several books. Secondly, we invited visitors to leave a short comment. Fourteen of the eighteen people who chose to comment said the instructions were clear. Two comments mentioned that the shelf was difficult to reach when the nearby reading table was busy.

We must be careful about treating those comments as representative. Some visitors did not leave a comment, and we did not ask people who had never tried the exchange. In other words, the feedback can help us improve the shelf, but it cannot tell us what everyone in the neighbourhood thinks. One volunteer suggested opening another exchange point at the sports centre. That might be worth considering later, although nobody has checked whether the sports centre has suitable space or staff.

For the next month, I would recommend keeping the existing shelf and testing a different position for the reading table, subject to the library manager's agreement. We can ask both users and non-users about accessibility. After that, we can decide whether an additional exchange point is justified. We should not announce an expansion before we understand the practical requirements.
