Eva: I'd like to make the service more consistent. Last month, people who came to the advice desk were sometimes referred to different volunteers for the same kind of request. A shared glossary might help volunteers give the same explanation of a technical term. I don't mean that every conversation should follow a fixed script.
Farid: I was about to ask about that. When someone has already explained their situation twice, consistency can mean not having to start again. A glossary might help with wording, but it wouldn't necessarily preserve the history of the enquiry. I would rather test whether a brief note, agreed with the visitor, makes follow-up easier.
June: I hear two purposes there. We also need to avoid implying that a shared note is automatically available to everyone who works at the desk. The visitor would need to understand who could see it and agree to its use. A wording tool and a record of an individual's situation need different safeguards.
Eva: Yes, I wasn't proposing a shared record of individual cases. I still think a small glossary is worth examining, but I can see why a measure of consistent wording wouldn't tell us whether a visitor has had to repeat their story.
Farid: And I wouldn't judge the glossary by that measure either. Could we first find examples of terms volunteers interpret differently, using invented examples rather than actual visitors' information? Separately, we could ask what a safe follow-up process would involve.
June: That seems a reasonable design enquiry. We haven't approved a glossary for public use or a new record system. I'd suggest drafting the two questions and asking the service lead which approvals would be needed before any trial.
Eva: Agreed. Let's keep the two purposes visible in the plan rather than call both of them consistency and leave it at that.
